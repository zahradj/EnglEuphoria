# Academy fast-progress evidence, part A (retrieval, vocabulary, output, feedback, grammar)

Date: 2026-10-10. Scope: 1:1 online, teens 11-18, A1-B1, 60-minute lesson plus a 10-minute Daily 10.

**Method and honesty note.** I searched the web and could read search-surfaced abstracts and summaries. Direct page fetches of publisher sites (Wiley, Cambridge) failed in this environment (DNS error), so I could NOT read the full papers. Every number below was seen in a search result (abstract or secondary summary). Anything I could not see is marked **UNVERIFIED**. Before public use, check each number against the paper. Quality labels judge the evidence for OUR setting, not just the study.

**Corrections to `docs/academy-learning-science.md`:**
- "Yanagisawa & Webb 2021 (SSLA, 42 studies)" mixes two papers. The involvement-load meta-analysis is Yanagisawa & Webb (2021), *Language Learning* 71(2), 487-536. The 42-study model is "Involvement Load Hypothesis Plus", *SSLA* 44 (2022), 1279-1308 (a correction notice is attached to it).
- The spacing meta-analysis is now identified: Kim & Webb (2022), *Language Learning* 72(1), 269-319, 37 studies, 48 experiments. The "98 effect sizes, 48 experiments" thesis (Kim, Western Ontario, N = 3,411) looks like the same dataset, but I could not confirm that.
- Rowland 2014 g about 0.50 was not re-checked here. Yang et al. 2021 was re-verified (below).
- The "+1/+3/+7/+21/+60 days" schedule remains a rule of thumb. No study I found validates those exact intervals for teens.

## Design rules we should adopt

1. **Space reviews across days, simple schedule, ending in a delayed check.** Evidence: Kim & Webb 2022 (spacing helps; equal = expanding; longer gaps win on delayed tests), Cepeda 2008 (gap about 20% of the wanted retention interval for weeks-long goals). Keep the +1/+3/+7/+21 ladder as a default, mark it an assumption. Confidence: high that spacing helps, medium on the exact intervals.
2. **Retrieve with production first, feedback after every item, never just re-read.** Evidence: Yang 2021 (g = 0.499, bigger with feedback), Kim & Webb (immediate feedback g = 1.04). Confidence: high for the principle, medium for L2 teens.
3. **Make every Daily 10 item a production retrieval, with meaning cue (image/gloss) at first encounter and fading cues later.** Evidence: Zhang et al. (L1 + picture best on immediate tests, marginal on delayed), Chiu & Hawkins (weak). Confidence: medium.
4. **Count encounters across skills and varied uses, not one drill; no magic number.** Evidence: Uchihara 2019 (frequency is one of many variables), Yanagisawa & Webb (involvement load only partly predictive). Confidence: medium.
5. **Teach each target word or chunk deliberately, then require use in a task (choose, fit, justify) within the same lesson and again in the next.** Evidence: Webb et al. 2023 (incidental gains vary widely), ILH. Confidence: medium.
6. **Treat chunks as single items; test them in speaking.** Evidence: Boers 2006, Wood 2009 (flagged for replication). Confidence: low-medium.
7. **Take 2 comes in the same lesson shortly after Take 1, after feedback; plan-then-repeat is an option to measure.** Evidence: Abdi Tabari 2025 (repetition raises accuracy and syntactic complexity, shorter gaps better), D'Ely 2019 (n = 47). Confidence: medium. Expect lower complexity in some students; track it.
8. **Feedback ladder: prompts first (self-repair), then clue, then direct fix; use direct recasts only at A1 or for form-new items.** Evidence: Lyster & Saito 2010 (prompts better, durable, bigger for younger), Li 2010. Confidence: medium (small k, classroom).
9. **Timing: immediate in controlled form-focused practice, delayed (end of Take 1) in free speaking.** Evidence: Nassaji & Kartchava 2021 (both work), Li, Zhu & Ellis 2016 (immediate slight edge for grammar judgement; proficiency interaction). Confidence: low-medium; the delay in fluency tasks is a motivational choice more than a proven gain.
10. **Show the correction on screen as well as saying it (multimodal recast).** Evidence: Jung & Revesz 2024 (one study). Confidence: low.
11. **Grammar: noticing examples, then a short explicit rule (<= 3 lines), then controlled practice, then a free-use Take.** Evidence: Goo 2015, Spada & Tomita 2010 (explicit > implicit). Confidence: high for explicit rule, low for discovery-first as a speed gain.
12. **Log each learner's recurrent errors and re-test exactly those in Take 2 and in the next Remember?.** Evidence: none direct (inference from CF and retrieval evidence). Confidence: low; run as a pilot measure.

## What the evidence does NOT support / where it is thin

- No study tested our exact case (teens, 1:1 online tutor, 60-minute lesson plus Daily 10). Almost all pooled data are university-age, lab, or non-L2.
- Exact intervals (+1/+3/+7/+21/+60) and "how many successful retrievals make a word safe" have no published L2 answer that I found.
- Spacing evidence is mainly vocabulary; L2 grammar spacing is thin (Kim & Webb call for it).
- "8-12+ encounters" is not a threshold; frequency is one of many variables, and the repetition effect was smaller in school-age learners than in university students (via a summary, UNVERIFIED).
- Pictures are not proven better than L1 glosses for learning; they are good retrieval cues.
- Prompts beating recasts rests on 15 classroom studies; the effect is not established for 1:1 online. Recasts are not useless (medium effects).
- Immediate vs delayed feedback: no clear winner. Do not claim either.
- Guided discovery vs direct rule presentation: no pooled evidence for discovery being faster. Worked examples and error-pattern practice for L2 grammar: nothing found.
- A talk-time threshold that "matters" (60/70/75%): no evidence found; keep as an operating target.
- Planning meta-analysis (Johnson & Abdi Tabari): results not seen. Lambert/Kormos and Bui repetition/planning work: not found, not cited.
- Numbers that remain UNVERIFIED against original papers: Li 2010 immediate/delayed d values; Mackey & Goo overall interaction d; r = .34 (Uchihara 2019); Webb 2023 pooled gains; Kim & Webb g = 1.15 retention; Zhang et al. year and title.
