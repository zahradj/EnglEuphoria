# Research — how to make a lesson that reaches its objective (searched 2026-10-09)

Quality: [V] research paper, [P] practitioner/official site, [T] teardown/blog. Searches were short and several returned nothing on the exact question; gaps are stated.

| Topic | What was found | Quality | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| CEFR A1 can-do | The A1 global descriptor includes "can introduce him/herself and others and can ask and answer questions about personal details"; speaking has *interaction* (two-way) and *production* (alone); a short *rehearsed* statement, e.g. introducing a speaker, is A1. Sites: britishcouncil.es/en/english/levels/a1, osd.at, engxam. | [P] | Objective wording; exit task = rehearsed self-introduction + one asked-back question |
| ACTFL Novice | Novice Low = isolated words and memorised phrases; "I can introduce myself and provide basic personal information; I can say my name and ask someone else's name" is Novice Mid. LinguaFolio: "About me — I can write about myself using learned phrases and memorized expressions". | [P] | Level honesty: A1 = learned phrases with slots, not free speech |
| Can-do as objectives | Can-do statements are described as objectives that are clear, understandable and achievable. | [P] | Objective rules |
| PPP vs TBLT | Few empirical comparisons; Sheen (2006): Grade 6, 7 months, PPP did better on oral production and grammaticality; other studies favour TBLT for vocabulary use/retention or writing; evidence for true beginners is weak; Ellis et al. describe input-based tasks for beginners. | [V, mixed, small] | PPP-style scaffolding with a task at the end for A1 |
| Dialogue recitation | Beginning-level recitation of dialogues with Q&A drills: verbatim repetition helps recall of formulaic expressions (Durrant & Schmitt 2010 cited); short-term retention only tested; rote buys processing time, elaboration gives retention. | [V, small] | Read-and-repeat, then fade, then use in role-play (elaboration) |
| Shadowing | Busuu speaking = listen-and-repeat drills modelled on shadowing, with record-and-compare; no controlled study found in this search. | [P]/[T] | "Say it" step on each line; type-instead |
| ABA English | Each unit opens with a short film with native actors; then Speak (imitate the characters), Interpret (dub the actors, read, listen to yourself), Write, then grammar; 144 units, A1–C1. Marketing claims. | [P] | Story first as a scene, imitate the characters, grammar last |
| Babbel Speak | Choose a dialogue, review the tasks, take turns with a partner; repeat, slow down, translate, hint. | [P] | Turn-taking role-play with the cast |
| Duolingo | Easy recognition first, harder production later; picture matching, tap-what-you-hear, speaking from the first lesson; content writers draft sentences and mini-dialogues first, then exercises are built from that pool. | [P]/[T] | Dialogue pool first, many exercise types from it |
| Cambridge/Oxford A1 books | Unit 1 pattern: match greetings to pictures -> listen and fill the gaps in the dialogue -> choose the best order of introduction sentences -> mingle/pair role-play -> write about yourself. (Touchstone 1, English Unlimited Starter, Cambridge course 1.1.) | [P] | Practice ladder and exit task |
| Online 1:1 schools (Novakid, VIPKid) | Friendly first contact, a toy/prop as go-between, listen-and-repeat several times; rewards; 25-minute lessons with 5 min feedback. Teacher guides are behind logins. | [P] | Warm first 5 minutes; repeat-after-me; feedback built in |
| Free A1 "introduce yourself" lessons | Model paragraph (name, age, country, hobby) -> read -> gap-fill dialogue -> pairs ask each other -> write 5-6 sentences. | [P] | Same ladder; our exit task is the spoken/typed card |

## Limits (not hidden)
Most evidence is practitioner/official marketing; the PPP/TBLT studies are small and mixed; nothing found on fading cloze or shadowing as controlled studies; Duolingo's first-lesson content could not be confirmed; no teen-specific first-lesson trial found. Treat this as design guidance, not proof.
