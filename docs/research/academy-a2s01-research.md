# Research: A2 Season 1 "Last Weekend", E1 Cold Open "The Photo Wall" (2026-10-14)

Question: how do good A2 lessons open a unit about the past for teens, and in what order should was / were / -ed be met?
Method: five web searches (standard mode, 2026-10-14). Limits: the searches returned teacher resources (TES, LearnEnglishFeelGood, LessonUp, Cambridge English), not Duolingo / Busuu / Oxford app mechanics, and no British Council page. Mechanics are taken, never content.

| Source | Mechanic taken | Quality / limit |
|---|---|---|
| TES "Past simple regular verbs: story-based grammar and speaking lesson" | learners guess a character's hidden backstory; match sentences to characters, then deduce the rule (guided discovery); was / were is taught before regular -ed | teacher-made, not peer reviewed; good fit for our mystery frame |
| TES past simple vs continuous creepy story | put story pictures in order and guess what happened | single resource; we use plates and a photo wall instead of sequencing |
| LearnEnglishFeelGood simple-past plan | warm-up "What did you do yesterday?", presentation, controlled practice, speaking task; -ed pronunciation /t/ /d/ /ɪd/ | PDF plan, listening part unclear; -ed sounds are left for E2 / E3 |
| British Council LearnEnglish Kids grammar page and worksheet (past simple 'to be') | was with I/he/she/it, were with you/we/they; a short story text with true/false before gap-fill | kids site, but the rule wording is clear and short: our 3-line rule follows it |
| TES was/were listening lesson (five characters, where were they?) | two listening rounds, gist then detail; discover where characters were | paid resource, not A2-labelled; the 'where were you?' frame is our Explore |
| Guided-discovery sources (British Council definition, small classroom studies) | give examples first, let learners find the rule, then state it | small single-context studies: suggestive only; we state the rule in 3 lines right after the model, as the owner requires |
| Cambridge English A2 Key for Schools writing plan | story from three pictures | exam prep; informs the Release at E8, not E1 |

Decisions: (1) E1 is receptive: the student HEARS was / were / -ed in a story and chunks, sees the rule in three lines, and makes one guess ("I think Ava was at the lake") — production of the structure is E3. (2) New mechanic: the Explore "photo wall" (tap a photo, hear where Ava was). (3) Sequencing: context -> model -> rule -> two different games per word set -> sort own weekend -> Mission (Take 1, feedback, Take 2) -> teach-back, see `academy-lesson-sequencing.md`.

Not found (said plainly): a British Council LearnEnglish Teens listening page for was / were, an Oxford University Press post on this exact topic, and any description of how Duolingo or Busuu design their past-simple screens. Those mechanics are not claimed here. Benchmarks used: TES, British Council Kids, LearnEnglishFeelGood, Cambridge English (4); the new mechanic is the Explore photo wall.
