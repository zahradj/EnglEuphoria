# Research — B1 Season 1 "Have You Ever...?" (present perfect: experience) and lesson 1 (Cold Open), 2026-10-15

Why now: a one-to-one A2 student asked to revise the **past simple, the present perfect and the three verb forms** (base, past simple, past participle). The roadmap puts the present perfect at B1, and the three forms are never taught as a set (see the search of the curriculum, 2026-10-15). Method: web search on 2026-10-15; mechanics, structure and sequencing only, **never content**. The search tool returned summaries, so each row says what was and was not verified. Nothing here is copied; the lesson text will be written fresh.

## 4. Typical difficulties of our students' home languages (ar, es, fr, tr, it)

| Source | What was taken | Quality / limits | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Almishwat 2023 (PhD, Arab learners)](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/34396/1/Almishwat_GA_Linguistics_PhD_2023%20.pdf), [JLTR, Arab learners and the present perfect](https://jltr.academypublication.com/index.php/jltr/article/view/6653) | Arabic has no direct equivalent: learners replace the present perfect with the simple past or present and lose the "up to now" meaning; the hardest English tense for the group. | Academic; strongest evidence of the five languages. | partly verified |
| [Language transfer chart (Palomar College)](https://pages.palomar.edu/eminamide/wp-content/uploads/sites/36/2013/02/Language-Transfer-Chart-ESL.pdf) | "Simple past used for present perfect" listed for Arabic, French, Italian, Spanish and Turkish (among others). French and Italian use a *compound past* for ordinary past events, so *I have seen him yesterday* is a likely error; Spanish has a present perfect, so form transfers well but the use with a stated time may over-extend. | A teaching chart, not a controlled study; **no dedicated French, Italian or Turkish study found**. | unverified for fr, it, tr |

|---|---|---|---|

| Harmer's Engage-Study-Activate ([overview](https://www.barefootteflteacher.com/p/what-is-engage-study-activate), [PPP vs ESA vs TBL](https://www.myenglishpages.com/esl-lesson-planning-frameworks/)) | Stages can be repeated and re-ordered; Study covers meaning, form, pronunciation; Activate is freer use. | [Cambridge CELTA frameworks (handouts)](https://www.scribd.com/document/510544588/Lesson-Frameworks) | Lead-in to context, then clarification of meaning, form and pronunciation, then controlled and freer practice, then feedback; frameworks are guidelines adapted to level, length and aim. | Training handouts, not the official syllabus. | unverified |
