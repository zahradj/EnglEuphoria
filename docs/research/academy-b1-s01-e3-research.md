**Video-game mechanic: Detective board + deduction log** (*Return of the Obra Dinn*, `docs/academy-video-game-mechanics.md`): the student pins evidence sentences to a case board and fills a log (who / has been where / when) from them. Evidence for classroom use: **none found** for this exact mechanic [U]; closest sources are the commercial grammar-mystery kits above. So the board is our own design and must be piloted; no effect is claimed.

## 3. What this changes in the plan

- Task first, rule after: the student sorts and tests evidence **before** the three-line rule is stated (guided discovery, as in the B1 plan above).
- The past simple is shown **only as the follow-up** ("When did you go?"), never as a second thing to learn from scratch (it is known from lesson 1).
- *been / gone* gets its own short sort, because the sources agree it is the point learners miss.
- No murder or crime theme; the suspects are club members and the "crime" is a missing postcard.
- Open: EGP and GSE descriptors not checked; no L1 research found for the five home languages.
