# Games that suit our Pre-A1 learners (ages ~4–7) — research, 2026-10-05

Why: the owner saw the N Dash (U4L2) was "too fast", "hard to pay attention and focus and make it right".
Question: which game mechanics actually suit 4–7-year-old non-readers on a tablet / laptop, and which of ours don't?

## What the research says

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| **Tap** is the most intuitive gesture for preschoolers; draw/trace, swipe (with a visual cue), drag and slide also work. Pinch, tilt/shake, multi-touch, flick and double-tap are the least intuitive. Children can drag an object to a place but struggle to keep the finger on the screen, so partial completion should still count. | Sesame Workshop / Joan Ganz Cooney Center, *Best Practices: Designing Touch Tablet Experiences for Preschoolers* (2012) |
| Preschoolers rest their wrists on the **bottom edge** of the tablet: hotspots along the edge get hit by accident. They hold tablets in landscape. | same |
| Non-readers need **spoken instructions and visual cues**, never text-only. | same |
| Time-outs: re-prompt after **3–5 s** idle in stories, **6–8 s** in games. A wrong answer is a chance to **restate the goal, hint, and highlight the right answer until the child picks it**. | same (summarised in UX reviews) |
| Ages 4–7 do better with **large targets** (≥ 48 dp) and **gaps** between them (~64 px); crowded screens confuse 2–4-year-olds. Attention span 8–10 min → short rounds, frequent small rewards. | aufaitux.com, ungrammary.com UX guidelines for children; NN/g "Design for kids based on physical development" |
| In a study of 170 children aged 2–4, **dragging** an object to learn its name produced more word learning than **tapping** it for children from low-SES families — tapping is a reflex that needs little attention. Extra hotspots/pop-ups can **distract** from learning. | Kirkorian et al. / Russo-Johnson et al., *All Tapped Out: Touchscreen Interactivity and Young Children's Word Learning* (2017) |
| Khan Academy Kids (ages 2–8): **self-paced, gentle pacing, no pressure**. Lingokids: rich but "can be a bit too much" (highly stimulating). Duolingo ABC: tap, drag, listen, trace, speak — short sets. | App Store / Good Play Guide / Screenwise reviews |
| Very young learners (British Council): songs, chants, TPR, simple flashcard games (Tower Game, Sit Down Game), arts and crafts; **calm TPR** ("touch your nose if you're ready") to refocus. Cambridge Pre A1 listening: **put small pictures in the right place in a big picture**. | British Council *How young children learn English through play*; TeachingEnglish *Managing very young learners*; Cambridge English Pre A1 activities |

## Rules for our Pre-A1 games (from the research)

1. **No clock, no race, no falling or moving targets** by default. A child this age should never lose because they were slow.
2. **One decision at a time**: 2–4 big, still choices, spoken prompt, 🔊 to hear it again.
3. **Deliberate actions teach better than reflex taps**: "pick it, then put it somewhere" (into a basket, into the sand, onto the face) beats "tap fast".
4. **Wrong = teach, not punish**: name what they tapped, repeat the goal, and after 2 misses **glow the right answer** until it's chosen. No hearts lost.
5. **Idle help**: if nothing is tapped for ~7 s, replay the prompt (and after a second idle, wiggle the answer).
6. **Keep controls away from the bottom edge** (wrist zone) and away from each other.
7. **Short**: 4–6 rounds per game, a visible reward that stays (stars, prints, stickers, a finished picture).
8. Text is a caption for the adult only — every instruction is spoken.

## Our games, audited (Pre-A1 library)

| Game | Used in | Problem for 4–7 | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dash` (catch the right letter while things run past) | 11 scenes | timed race, moving targets, reflex taps | replace with `sound-pick` (calm, done in U4L2) or make a calm mode |
| `catch-sort` (falling items into 2 baskets) | 5 | falling targets + timer | calm mode: items wait on a shelf, drag/tap into the basket, no timer |
| `sound-pop` (pop bubbles with the sound) | 6 | timer, moving bubbles | calm mode: bubbles float slowly and **wait**; no timer |
| `rapid-recall` (quick fire) | 3 | clock | remove the clock; keep "what's this?" one at a time |
| `brick-crush` | 4 | clock | remove the clock |
| `shape-fishing` | 4 | moving fish | slow the fish down and let a tap "hook" any fish for 1 s |
| `simon-touch`, `tpr-actions` | — | gentle countdown ring | OK: the ring is a pace for *moving the body*, not a race; keep it long (≥ 5 s) |

Good fits already in the library (keep using): `sound-pick`, `face-builder`, `train-recall` (what's missing), `memory` (4–6 cards), `tick-cross`, `lift-flap`, `odd-one-out`, `story-order`, `spin-wheel`, `basket` (drag into the basket), `trace`, songs + TPR, `listen-repeat-cards`.

## New mechanics worth building (research-backed, calm)

- **Listen and place** (Cambridge Pre A1 Listening Part 1 style): "Put the fish in the sea" — drag a picture into a big scene; deliberate, untimed. *(Our `sand-prints` in U4L3 is this pattern.)*
- **Feed the character**: drag the named food/item into a character's mouth (one item per round).
- **Peekaboo / lift-the-flap** hunts with 3–4 flaps.
- **Sticker scene**: the child builds a picture by placing the words they hear; the picture is theirs at the end.
- **Built for U2L3 (2026-10-06), all calm:** `shape-magic` Magic Pencil (trace the shape, it comes alive in the picture), `shape-peek` What's Peeking? (feely-bag guessing, each miss reveals more), `shape-sorter` Shape Sorter (hear colour + shape, post the block), `shape-bubbles` Bubble Pop (bubbles float in place and wait).
- **Calm TPR "ready" routine**: "If you're ready, touch your nose!" before a game to refocus.
