# Academy: teen SLA findings, L1 transfer gaps and what the curriculum must schedule

Research note, 2026-10-14. Companion to `docs/academy-learning-science.md` (read first; its techniques, tutoring figure of 0.29 SD, and "claims we will not make" are NOT repeated here). Hub: Academy only (ages 13-18, one-to-one, 60 min). Home languages: Arabic, Spanish, French, Turkish, Italian.

## How to read the marks

- **[V]** verified = the claim appeared in a search result seen in this session (abstract, snippet or summary page). It is not the same as "read the full paper".
- **[V-weak]** = seen, but only in teaching blogs, tutor pages or small theses; treat as practical guidance, not research.
- **[K]** = established literature known from memory, NOT re-checked this session.
- **[U]** = unverified; do not cite publicly.
- Search tool returned snippets only; no full papers were opened. Every figure below must be checked against the original before any public or parent-facing use.
- Most L1 sources are contrastive analyses, small studies or teaching sites. Error *types* are well attested; error *rates* are not.

## Part 1. Evidence on teaching English to adolescents

Items already covered in `academy-learning-science.md` (retrieval, spacing, interleaving limits, corrective feedback ladder, HVPT, shadowing, anxiety) are not restated. Only new or sharpened points follow.

### 1.1 One-to-one tutoring effect

| Claim | Source | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| Published pooled effect of tutoring on PreK-12 learning is 0.288 SD (89 RCTs); the 0.37 SD figure belongs to the 2020 working paper. | https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/promise-tutoring-prek-12-learning-systematic-review-and-metaanalysis-experimental-evidence ; https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00028312231208687 ; https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476 | [V] |
| Effects are larger with teacher/paraprofessional tutors, earlier grades, >= 3 days a week, during school hours. | Stanford NSSA summary above | [V] |
| These are mostly maths/reading tutoring studies, not English-as-a-foreign-language one-to-one. | inferred from the same summary | [V] (limit) |

Implication: frequency matters. A student at 1 session a week is outside the best-supported tutoring condition; the Daily 10 has to carry the other days. Do not claim a "tutoring effect" for Academy in marketing.

### 1.2 Output hypothesis (Swain) and pushed output

| Claim | Source | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| Swain proposed three functions of output: noticing, hypothesis testing, metalinguistic reflection. | https://todoele.net/node/445081 (summary page) | [V-weak] |
| A research review (Shehadeh, "Comprehensible output, from occurrence to acquisition") said that after a decade of study there was still a severe lack of data showing that output causes L2 acquisition; research was mostly descriptive. | https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/comprehensible-output-from-occurrence-to-acquisition-an-agenda-fo/ | [V] (old; field has moved on [K]) |
| Pushed output is confounded with feedback in most studies, so its separate effect is hard to isolate. | search summary, same result set | [V-weak] |
| Mackey & Goo 2007 interaction meta-analysis supports interaction for learning. | not retrieved | [K], effect size [U] |

Implication: output is a strong theory, not a proven independent lever. Keep "Take 1 -> feedback -> Take 2" (which is output + feedback + repetition) rather than selling "speaking more" as the mechanism.

### 1.3 Vocabulary load

| Claim | Source | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| For authentic written text, ~8,000-9,000 word families are needed for 98% coverage; for spoken text ~6,000-7,000 families for 98%; ~3,000 families give ~95% of spoken text (Nation 2006). | summary of Nation 2006 via https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1317203.pdf ; https://www.lextutor.ca/cv/laufer_cobb_2019.pdf ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/how-much-vocabulary-is-needed-to-use-english-replication-of-van-zeeland-schmitt-2012-nation-2006-and-cobb-2007/1D217A56A2E0056E67802A6A8360FDDE | [V] via secondary text |
| Graded readers: ~2,000 families for 95%, ~3,000 for 98% (Webb & Macalister 2013). | search summary (Webb 2008 "Evaluating vocabulary load" page https://vuw-web.matrix.squiz.cloud/lals/resources/paul-nations-resources/paul-nations-publications/publications/documents/2008-Webb-Evaluating-vocabulary-load.pdf) | [V-weak] (figure from a secondary summary) |
| Whether 98% is the right threshold for speech is contested. | same search summary | [V-weak] |
| An "ideal words per hour" figure for a 60-min lesson. | no source found | [U] |

Implication: there is no evidence-based "words per hour". Size is the long-run driver: A1->C1 means thousands of families, so the weekly load must come mainly from Daily 10 and input, not from stuffing the live hour. Keep the live-hour new-item budget as a design assumption and measure it in the pilot.

### 1.4 Pronunciation and intelligibility

| Claim | Source | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| Munro & Derwing (1995) separated intelligibility, comprehensibility and accentedness as partly independent; accent can be strong while speech is fully understood. | https://iastate.pressbooks.pub/teachingpronunciation/chapter/chapter-2-pronunciation-in-language-teaching/ ; https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2020.626985/pdf ; https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/books/9789027257635 (2022 volume) | [V] |
| Levis's Intelligibility Principle: teach what most threatens being understood, not accent removal. | same | [V] |
| Researchers do not agree on exactly which features matter most for intelligibility; measuring gains is hard. | same | [V] |
| Teachers and learners still want native-like accent, which conflicts with the research. | Frontiers review above | [V] |
| Functional-load ranking of contrasts (which minimal pairs matter most). | not retrieved | [K] (Brown, Catford) / [U] here |

Implication, and a tension with the repo: `CLAUDE.md` demands "no accent" for the *voices students hear* (models). That is a model-quality rule and stays. For the *student's* own speech the goal is comprehensibility, not erasing accent; scoring and feedback must say so.

### 1.5 Motivation, identity, willingness to communicate (teens)

| Claim | Source | Mark |
|---|---|---|

| In one Norwegian lower-secondary thesis, ideal L2 self correlated positively with WTC; ought-to self did not. | https://hvlopen.brage.unit.no/hvlopen-xmlui/handle/11250/3019144?show=full | [V-weak] (one master's thesis) |

| Dornyei's L2 Motivational Self System (ideal L2 self, ought-to self, experience). | not re-checked | [K] |

Implication: tie each Season to a self the teen wants (travel, gaming, study abroad, friends online) and let the student choose the skin; do not rely on "parent-pressure" framing (ought-to self showed no WTC link in the one study seen).

## Part 2. L1-specific difficulties and what to schedule

General caveat: all rows below are error *tendencies* from contrastive analysis and small studies. They tell us what to *screen for in the first sessions*, not what every student does. Each student's Mistake Bank overrides this table.

### 2.6 What the curriculum should schedule because of these

Derived by us from 2.1-2.5 and the intelligibility principle (1.4). These are proposals; nothing here is a validated sequence.

| Item | Why | Which L1s | Where to place |
|---|---|---|---|
| /ɪ/ vs /iː/ (ship/sheep, live/leave) | Spanish evidence shows intelligibility cost; Turkish, Italian, Arabic reports of lax-vowel trouble | es, tr, it, ar | A1 (early Seasons); minimal-pair listening with several voices (`speechPolicy.ts` voices) |
| /p/ vs /b/ | Arabic; mostly Arabic | ar | A1 first 2 Seasons |
| /b/ vs /v/ | Spanish (and some Arabic/Turkish dialect speakers [U]) | es | A1-A2 |
| Initial s-clusters (no prothetic e-/epenthesis) | Spanish e-, Arabic epenthesis | es, ar | A1-A2, tied to school/sport/street words |
| th /θ ð/ | French, Turkish, Italian | fr, tr, it | A1 awareness, A2 practice; accept /f, s, t/ as non-blocking at first [RT] |
| h | French dropping/overuse | fr | A1 |
| Word stress and schwa | French final stress, Italian even weight | fr, it | from A2: 2-syllable stress rule, then -tion/-ity families |
| Double letters not doubled in sound | Italian | it | A1-A2, spelling-linked |
| Copula "be" in every present sentence | Arabic omission; also zero-copula in other L1s [U] | ar | A1 from Season 1 (rule + teach-back) |
| Articles a/the | Turkish and Arabic reports; Spanish/French/Italian have articles with different rules | tr, ar | A1-B1 recycled each Season |
| SVO order, verb right after subject | Turkish SOV | tr | A1 and again with questions/subordinate clauses at A2 |
| Spelling: short vowels, silent letters, sound-spelling patterns | Arabic vowel-spelling errors; Turkish read-as-written | ar, tr | every Season, 3-4 words/Season in Daily 10 dictation |
| False friends (embarrassed, actually, sensible/sensitive, library, ...) | Cross-language | es, fr, it | at the Season where each word appears; flag in Word Lab, reuse in Mistake Bank |

## Part 3. Known gaps in learner progress

| Gap | Evidence | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| Intermediate plateau: learners keep understanding more while production stalls; remedy debated (volume of input vs structured output). | https://www.eslpod.com/research/intermediate-plateau/ ; https://ielts.idp.com/singapore/about/news-and-articles/article-overcome-intermediate-plateau-for-learning-english/en-gb ; https://skyteach.ru/tefl/getting-over-the-intermediate-plateau/ | [V-weak] (practitioner articles; Richards' work cited second-hand) |
| Productive vocabulary lags receptive: ~10-15 encounters to recognise a new word (Pellicer-Sanchez & Schmitt 2010), more for production (Webb 2007). | https://howwelearnlanguages.substack.com/p/reading-but-not-improving (secondary blog) | [V-weak]; figures [U] until checked |
| Incidental vocabulary uptake from exposure is low (see existing doc §2 row 6). | `academy-learning-science.md` | already recorded |
| The "no source for 8-12 encounters" correction in the existing doc stays: do not hard-code encounter counts. | `academy-learning-science.md` §10 | already recorded |
| Speaking confidence: anxiety correlates negatively with achievement (r = -0.36) and WTC depends on perceived competence, motivation, anxiety. | existing doc row 14 ; MASEM link in 1.5 | [V] (correlational) |
| A2/B1 plateau being specifically age-13-18. | no teen-specific source | [U] |

Implication: the plateau risk is a production and breadth issue. B1 should shift weight from "more new words" to (a) forcing production of words already recognised, (b) widening range (collocations, connectors, register), (c) fluency tasks with repetition, and (d) measuring active vs passive items separately.

## Curriculum rules to adopt (each actionable for a Season spec)

1. **L1 screen in Season 1, Episode 1-2.** Run a 5-minute listening/saying probe (p/b, b/v, ship/sheep, s-cluster, th, h) and a 1-sentence copula/word-order writing sample; record in the learner profile as L1-risk flags that seed the Mistake Bank. Basis: Part 2 error tendencies are widely attested but individual.
2. **One named pronunciation focus per Season** (2 minutes of the Word Lab, plus 1 Daily-10 item per day), chosen from the Part 2.6 table and ordered by intelligibility cost, not by "accent": A1 S1-S2 /ɪ/-/iː/ and copula "be"; /p/-/b/ and h; S3-S4 s-clusters and th (awareness); A2 word stress + schwa, /b/-/v/; B1 sentence stress, linking, intonation for attitude. A student's own flags can swap the Season focus.
3. **Perception before production, several voices.** Every pronunciation focus opens with minimal-pair listening using the approved voices (`speechPolicy.ts`), then production. Never browser TTS. (Matches existing HVPT row.)
4. **Score student speech for comprehensibility, not accent.** Rubrics (E8 checkpoint, Season portfolio) include "understood without effort" and ban "native-like accent" language. Voice *models* stay standard American per CLAUDE.md.
5. **Rule + teach-back includes an L1 contrast line where relevant.** E.g. "English needs a verb: I AM a student." Keep within the 3-line rule limit; the contrast is spoken by the teacher or shown in the interface language (translated chrome only), lesson content stays English.
6. **Copula, articles and word order are recycled every Season through A2**, one sentence-level production item each in Remember? and the Mistake Bank; add the SVO "verb after subject" check for all students but prioritise for Turkish.
7. **Spelling strand in every Season:** 3-4 target words dictated or typed from audio in Daily 10; vowel-letter patterns and silent letters for Arabic- and Turkish-L1 flags; doubled-letter sound check for Italian. Spelling errors are logged as a separate error type.
8. **False-friend list per Season, drawn from the Season's own words.** For each Season word with a known es/fr/it false friend (verify in a dictionary first), add a "Don't be fooled" card in Word Lab and a retrieval item two sessions later. Do not publish a false-friend table without dictionary verification (Italian list is currently unverified).
9. **Active-vs-passive vocabulary ledger.** Track each item as recognised vs produced (existing item states); a Season is not "done" for an item until it has been *produced* in the Mission or a Daily-10 production task. Do not assert encounter counts.
10. **Do not add "words per hour" quotas on evidence grounds.** Keep the live-hour new-item budget as a design assumption, record it in the pilot, and put extra volume into Daily 10 and input (graded text at roughly 95-98% known).
11. **Plateau guard from late A2.** From the last A2 Season: per Season, at least one Mission with a repeated-task Take 2 that adds a constraint (new connector, new collocation), one range target (e.g. 5 collocations or connectors to produce), and a fluency task with repetition. Report speaking range, not only accuracy.
12. **Frequency-aware homework.** For 1 session/week students, the Daily 10 is mandatory-by-default and the spacing rule uses calendar days (existing); show parents that frequency, not length, is what the tutoring evidence supports. Never claim a tutoring effect size in marketing.
13. **Teen-identity hook per Season.** Each Season spec names the "future self" it serves (ideal L2 self: trips, gaming, creators, studies) and one student-choice point (skin or task). Avoid ought-to framing ("your parents want...").
14. **Anxiety-safe speaking path.** Keep private rehearsal and Take 1 uncorrected; track a once-per-Season confidence rating (can-do self-rating) and compare to rubric scores.
15. **Items to verify before any public claim:** Pellicer-Sanchez & Schmitt 2010 and Webb 2007 encounter counts; Nation 2006 figures from the original; Gleason 2011 (n = 2) not generalised; Italian final-vowel transfer to learner speech; Turkish vowel-harmony transfer; all L1 error rates.

## Home-language watch lists in the blueprint (2026-10-16)

`src/curriculum/academy/l1Watch.ts` now holds 2-4 "listen for this" entries per Season (languages ar, es, fr, tr, it; what to hear, what to do, evidence mark V / V-weak / K) and every lesson blueprint carries its Season's list (`l1Watch`). **A1 (12 Seasons) is done**; A2 to C1 follow in the same shape, one level at a time. Entries marked K are standard contrastive linguistics that this research round did not re-check in a source; entries marked V-weak come from teaching sources in Part 2 above. They are tendencies to screen for, never facts about a student; the Mistake Bank overrides them. `l1Watch.test.ts` keeps the shape.
