# Research — one Season theme, eight related, progressive and systematic episodes (2026-10-16)

Question (owner, 2026-10-16): "the season theme should be one, and the episodes should be related to this, but progressive and systematic." Method: 12 web searches on 2026-10-16, result summaries only (nothing read in full). **Mechanics, structure and sequencing only, never content.** Marks: **[V]** a result stated it this round · **[S]** secondary/summary source · **[U]** not verified. Evidence for chaptered unit arcs in language learning is mostly frameworks and descriptive studies, not controlled trials; the design below is a reasoned synthesis, not a proven recipe.

## 2. What the Academy design takes from this

1. **A Season is a backwards-designed unit.** One theme sentence, one essential question the student can answer in English by the end, and the Release as the performance task. The eight episodes exist to make the Release possible (ACTFL/UbD).
2. **Each episode is a chapter of the theme, not the same theme restated.** Give every episode its own sub-question inside the theme, so the theme grows (meet it, name it, say it, use it in life, read about it, choose your angle, test it, show it).
3. **Progress along task complexity, one dimension per step** (Robinson): from recognising, to controlled production, to guided speaking, to free speaking with planning, to a longer or more reasoning-heavy task, ending with the Release as the most complex task. Never raise two dimensions in one episode.
4. **Each episode hands a question to the next** (open loop = the clue, E1 to E8). Evidence for cliffhangers is weak, so it is a motivation device, not a learning claim.
5. **Stay thematic, avoid semantic clustering.** Within one lesson teach words that co-occur in the theme's situations (thematic), not several same-category items or near-synonyms (semantic sets) at once; separate easily confused items across lessons.
6. **Guard against the thematic traps:** recycle earlier Seasons on schedule (`recycleFrom`, Remember?, Daily 10), and let tasks use language from outside the word list.
7. **Four strands across the eight episodes:** input, output, language-focus and fluency each appear; fluency episodes (E7 Remix, E8 Finale) use only known language.
8. **Anchor to can-do objectives that recur** across Seasons in new contexts (GSE practice): the spiral is built by the recycle plan, not by repeating the lesson.
