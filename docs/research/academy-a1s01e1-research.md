## Evidence

| Claim | Source | Use in the lesson |
|---|---|---|
| ≈ 95% lexical coverage = minimum, ≈ 98% = adequate unassisted comprehension; thresholds are on a sliding scale (Schmitt, Jiang & Grabe 2011 found a linear relation 90–100%). | Hu & Nation 2000; Laufer 1989; Laufer & Ravenhorst-Kalovski 2010; Schmitt et al. 2011 (via Language Teaching, Cambridge) [V]. Studies used intermediate learners, none on true beginners. | The Drop glosses every new word and adds almost no other unknown words; the validator counts glossed words as known. |
| Glossed texts → better word learning (≈ 45% vs 27% immediate) but little comprehension gain. | Yanagisawa et al. 2020 meta-analysis, summarised in PLoS ONE 2023 eye-tracking study [V]. | Gist checks kept separate from word meet; tap-to-gloss kept. |
| For beginning EFL learners, pre-teaching vocabulary helped listening comprehension more than schema activation. | Japanese high-school EFL study (Yamanashi repository) [V, small]. | The 14 words are met in sets of 4 right after the Drop and recycled in the Energiser, Mission and Release. |
| Listen/read for gist first, detail second. | Teacher-training handouts [P]. | Gist checks before detail items. |
| Task repetition mostly improves fluency; accuracy/complexity effects vary by task. | Bygate 2001 and later studies [V, mixed]. | Take 2 repeats Take 1 with one addition. |

## Limits

First-lesson design for teens is practitioner knowledge, not trials. No source found on Busuu or Lingokids first-lesson flows. Duolingo design detail is from teardowns. Treat all of this as design guidance, not proof of results.
