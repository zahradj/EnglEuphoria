## 1. What the evidence says makes lessons work fast (and how sure we are)

| # | Lever | What the evidence supports | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Spaced retrieval with feedback** | Spacing beats massing for L2 vocabulary (Kim & Webb 2022, 37 studies; immediate g ≈ 0.76, feedback in practice ≈ 1.04); retrieval + feedback raises classroom achievement (Yang 2021, g ≈ 0.50, all subjects). Short gaps lose on delayed tests. | High for the principle; **medium** for our exact intervals (+1/+3/+7/+21 is an unvalidated assumption) |
| 2 | **Meaning first, then retrieval** | Picture + L1 gloss beats either alone on immediate tests (g ≈ 0.33); the edge shrinks later, pictures work mainly as retrieval cues. | Medium |
| 3 | **Deliberate teaching, then use** | Incidental pick-up alone is weak and varies a lot; involvement load only partly predicts gains; **there is no magic "8–12 encounters"**. Count varied uses across skills instead. | Medium |
| 4 | **Pushed output with the Take 1 → feedback → Take 2 loop** | Oral task repetition clearly raises accuracy and syntactic complexity, less so fluency (meta-analysis 2025); written repetition + feedback beats without. Effects are smaller in classrooms than labs. | Medium |
| 5 | **Specific corrective feedback** | Medium, durable effect (Li 2010); prompts ≥ recasts in classrooms (Lyster & Saito 2010); timing: no clear winner; show the correction on screen too. | Medium |
| 6 | **Explicit rule after noticing** | Explicit instruction beats implicit (Goo 2015, Spada & Tomita 2010). Nothing shows discovery-first is faster. | High for explicit; low for discovery-first |
| 7 | **Dose and structure of tutoring** | School tutoring ≈ 0.29 SD (Nickow et al. 2023, 89 RCTs); larger with ≥ 3 sessions a week and trained tutors; at scale about a third to a half of that (Kraft 2024). Bloom's "2 sigma" is not replicated: never cite it. | Medium-high for "frequency and structure matter" |
| 8 | **Hours** | Cambridge's guide is cumulative from zero: A2 ≈ 180–200 h, B1 ≈ 350–400 h (about 200 h per level). Guidelines, not promises; independent study is not counted. | Medium |
| 9 | **Practice between lessons** | Homework effects are small and mostly correlational; app practice looks good in meta-analyses but is biased and heterogeneous; streak claims are company data. | Low-medium |
| 10 | **Motivation by choice, not rank** | Gamification g ≈ 0.25–0.49 depending on outcome; leaderboards can hurt low-ranked learners; autonomy support helps. | Medium |
| 11 | **Low-stakes speaking** | Anxiety correlates with achievement (r ≈ −0.36), correlational only. | Medium (as a design reason) |
| 12 | **Feedback that informs, and delayed production evidence** | Feedback d ≈ 0.48 (heterogeneous); learning ≠ in-session performance. | Medium |
Not supported (do not build on it): talk-time percentages, "attention span" segment lengths, "10 minutes per grade" homework, streak magic, "2x faster", "B1 in N weeks".

## 2. Audit of the current blueprint against the evidence

| Blueprint feature | Verdict | Change proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Run of show 5·7·10·10·4·12·7·5, Remember? first, Mission with Take 1 → feedback → Take 2, Release, Wrap | **Keep.** It already matches levers 1, 4, 5, 12. | Name the part of each part that carries the lever (see §3). |
| Remember? 7 min, production first, due items by calendar days | Keep. | Start with the student's **own error list** (Mistake Bank) before due items; every item answered gets feedback; log the delayed result per item (7 / 21 days). Treat intervals as a setting to tune from our own data. |
| The Drop = comprehensible input | Keep. | **Add modelling** (owner, 2026-10-10: "I see activities, not modelling or vocabulary"): in E2/E6 Drop show each new word with an animated picture, meaning, example and clue, and in E1/E3/E4 show the language **in use** by the cast with the frame highlighted *before* any game asks for it. |
| Notice & Build = noticing then ≤ 3-line rule | Keep. | Meaning cue at first meeting, **fade it in retrieval**; controlled practice with immediate feedback. |
| Mission 12 min, Take 2 in the same lesson | Keep. | Teacher prompts first (prompt → clue → direct fix; direct recast at A1); delayed feedback for free speaking; corrections shown on screen. |
| Release 7 min private first | Keep. | Make it the **delayed-evidence sample**: model hidden; saved for the Season portfolio and the teacher. |
| Wrap 5 min | Keep. | End with the teacher's **error log** (3 recurring errors) → next lesson's Remember? list, plus the Daily 10 assignment. |
| 14 new items per lesson, 8 lessons per Season, progressive stack | Keep the cap; **do not claim a magic number** | Measure retention per item; if Known-after-21-days is below target, reduce items, not hours. |
| Sessions per week 1, 2 or 3 | **Weak point.** Dose and spacing are the strongest levers we control. | Recommend and sell a **rhythm of 2 lessons + Daily 10 on 5 days** per week; 1 per week is the minimum; 3 for fast-track. |
| Daily 10 (solo, 10 min) | Keep, but the evidence for homework is thin. | Make it retrieval-only and calendar-scheduled; no leaderboard; streak freezes; measure completion honestly. |
| Teacher-in-the-loop | Keep: tutoring works when structured and trained. | A short teacher script per lesson, a tutor-training checklist, and the plan drawer; check fidelity (did Take 2 happen? was feedback specific?). |
| Level checks / checkpoints | Keep. | Judge progress with delayed speaking/writing samples, not XP; compare the pilot with L2 effect-size norms (small ≈ 0.4, medium ≈ 0.7, large ≈ 1.0), not Cohen's defaults. |

## 3. The effective lesson template (one 60-minute lesson)

| Part | Min | Job (lever) | Student does | Deck slide types |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Check-in | 5 | Safety + energy; 3 quick questions in English (11) | Picks a feeling, answers 2 easy questions, sees today's mission | `title`, `brief`, `badge`, `convo` |
| Remember? | 7 | Retrieval of last lesson, **production first, errors first, feedback on every item** (1, 12) | Re-builds last lesson's sentences, sorts who-says-what | `whosaid` (after a replay), `phonechat`, `memory` |
| The Drop | 10 | Meaning-first input and **modelling** (2, 3) | Meets each word with a moving picture, meaning, example, clue; watches the cast do the language with the pattern card | `teach`, `model`, one first-try game |
| Notice & Build | 10 | Controlled practice with immediate feedback; short rule (5, 6) | Plays: lockers, number train, padlock, spelling, passport control, conversation wheel | game slides, each tagged to a success criterion |
| Energiser | 4 | Attention reset, zero stakes (10) | A quick game | `memory`, `findwrong` |
| Mission | 12 | Pushed output: dial → Take 1 → feedback → Take 2 (4, 5) | Chooses help (Chill/Normal/Push), says it, gets specific feedback, says it again | `dial`, `say` (open), `fade`, `say` |
| Release | 7 | Delayed-evidence sample, model hidden, private first (12) | Fills the card, says everything from memory | `card`, `say` (hidden) |
| Wrap | 5 | Can-do ticks, error log, Daily 10 (11, 12) | Ticks honestly, sees the clue for next time | `wrap` |
Rules for every lesson: one objective with 3–5 criteria; every game tagged to one; **nothing is asked that was not first shown (words taught, language modelled)**; no two neighbouring slides use the same game; every correct answer rewarded briefly, every wrong one answered with a specific hint; no ranking, no timers.

## 4. How progress is measured (so we can say "fast" honestly)

1. **Item level:** each word/chunk New → Learning → Known (correct production on 2 days) → Secure (correct after ≥ 21 days in a freer task). Target to tune with data: ≥ 80 % of a lesson's items Known at +7 days.
2. **Skill level:** a short **delayed speaking sample** and a **writing sample** at the end of each Season (E8), rated with the CEFR-referenced rubric; compare with the sample at the Season's start.
3. **Behaviour:** lessons attended per week, Daily 10 days, hints used, Take 2 completed, error-log items fixed.
4. **Pilot design:** 20–30 students on the 2 + Daily-10 rhythm; pre/post speaking and vocabulary delayed tests; report effect sizes against L2 norms; share results only after that.

## 5. Honest claims

May say: lessons use spaced retrieval, specific feedback and a repeat-with-feedback loop, which are the best-supported techniques; one-to-one tutoring averages about 0.3 SD in school trials and works best when frequent and structured; Cambridge's guide is about 200 guided hours per CEFR level (our own arithmetic: A1 = 80 lessons ≈ 80 h plus about 30 h of Daily 10 over 40 weeks at 2 lessons a week).
May not say: "2x faster", "2 sigma", "B1 in N weeks", any effect of gamification/streaks/anxiety features, any number from the evidence logs tagged [S2], [M] or [U].
