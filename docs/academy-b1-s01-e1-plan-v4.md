## 1. Analysis (before any activity)
- **Student:** 13-18, has met the present perfect and irregular verbs at school but mixes "I went" and "I have been"; may know the words but not the choice. L1 difficulty (ar, es, fr, tr, it): the tenses map differently (French/Italian passé composé / passato prossimo cover finished time, so students over-use "have + participle" for finished events; Arabic and Turkish have no direct perfect).
- **Objective (blueprint):** I can understand the main idea of a short text about experiences and bucket lists. **Success criteria (kept, they are the lesson's contract):** C1 follow a blog: main idea, detail, what is not said. C2 six words (bucket list, dare, give it a go, once in a lifetime, tick it off, brave). C3 three forms of nine verbs. C4 choose past simple vs present perfect; write a question, an answer, a follow-up. C5 speak about a minute and write three sentences about my own list.
- **Skills needed:** reading (C1), vocabulary (C2), grammar + form (C3, C4), speaking (C4, C5), a little writing (C5). Listening is carried by the recorded cast voices, not a separate act.
- **Newness:** partly known. So the approach is **test-teach-test** (the student does a cold task first, we teach only the gap, they retake it and see the difference), with a **skills (pre / while / post)** reading act, and **discovery** for the rule (the student states it before we do). Not PPP: it would re-teach what school already did. Reason logged here as the plan requires.
- **Shape:** `ttt` (docs/academy-lesson-shapes.md). Declared on the script.

## 2. The idea: "The Dare Deck"
Vee dares you to build your own bucket list in an hour. Every activity you win drops a **Dare card** (a real thing you could do: "swim with dolphins", "learn the guitar"). Your deck is your final bucket list, read out in the Release. Frame, not a skeleton: the order below is this lesson's own.
