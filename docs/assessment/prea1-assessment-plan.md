# Pre-A1 assessment plan — where we test, what we test, and who moves on

**Status: proposal for the owner (2026-10-12).** Nothing here is built yet except the Unit 10 Boss Test (U10L7 "Action Hero"),
which is the model for the unit tests below.

## 1. What the research says (and what we take from it)

| Source | Finding | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| Cambridge Pre A1 Starters (official test format) | Three parts — Listening (4 parts, 20 questions, each heard twice), Reading & Writing (5 parts, 25 q.), Speaking (3–5 min, 4 parts: point to things in a big picture, answer about object cards, personal questions). Each part is reported as 1–5 **shields**, 15 in all. **There is no pass / fail**; the shields show strengths. | Our exit test copies the shape (listening + speaking + "early reading" = first sounds and blending, because our Pre-A1 children don't read yet) and reports per skill, not one score. |
| Cambridge YLE shields (school / centre guidance; *not* an official Cambridge rule) | Children with about 11–12 of 15 shields "may be ready" for the next level. | We use a clear bar per skill (below), set a little on the safe side. A teacher confirms every move up. |
| CEFR Companion Volume (2018/2020) and the CoE young-learner descriptors | Pre-A1 is "about half-way to A1": a small set of words and set phrases; e.g. can say name, age, family in simple words; understands very short, slow, clear speech with pictures. | Every test item is written from a Pre-A1 can-do statement we actually taught (see §4). |
| Mastery learning (Bloom; Block & Anderson; Slavin 1987 review) | Keep a mastery bar (≈ 80 %), check often, and give **corrective work that is different from the first teaching**; the evidence for big long-term gains is mixed, so keep checks short and frequent rather than adding big exams. | Short unit Boss Tests with an 80 % bar per skill, a "Practise again" pack (different games) for any skill below the bar — not a repeat of the whole unit. |
| British Council / TeachingEnglish "Assessing young learners", Lynda Taylor; McKay; Cameron | Young children: short, game-like, picture-based tasks, listening and speaking first, low anxiety, errors are normal; a bad first test experience can spoil attitudes to learning; observe a few things well rather than everything. | Tests look like games (heroes, treasure), one question at a time, spoken prompts, no clock, no "fail" screen; the teacher observes speaking with a 4-point rubric. |
| Our own platform (placement test redesign, `level_change_requests`) | Every placement is provisional until a teacher confirms it; a move to another CEFR level goes teacher request → admin approval. | Test results are evidence for that same path: the exit test fills in a level change request, the teacher confirms, the admin approves. |

## 2. Where the assessments go (decision)

```
Unit 1 → Unit 2 → Unit 3 → Unit 4 → Unit 5 ──► CHECKPOINT 1  "Halfway Explorer"
  (each unit ends with its Lesson 7 Boss Test)       (Units 1-5: me and my world)
Unit 6 → Unit 7 → Unit 8 → Unit 9 → Unit 10 ──► FINAL TEST  "Explorer Graduation"  ──► A1 (Welcome Town)
  (each unit ends with its Lesson 7 Boss Test)       (all of Pre-A1)
```

**Why after Unit 5 (not 4):** the ten units fall into two halves with different jobs. Units 1–5 are about **me** — hello and
feelings, colours and shapes, toys, my body, my family — which is exactly the CEFR Pre-A1 "describe myself (name, age, family)"
goal. Units 6–10 move to **the world around me** — house, animals, food, clothes, actions — and lean on what came before
(colours for clothes, "I have" for body and family, "I like" for food). Testing after Unit 5 checks the foundation before it is
built on, splits the level 5 + 5, and comes at a natural mid-course moment for parents. After Unit 4 would leave the family
unit and the "about me" goal out of the check.

Three layers, each with one job:

| Layer | When | Length | Job | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Remember?** warm-up + lesson games (exists) | every lesson | 2–3 min | keep words alive; formative | nothing stored, teacher sees it |
| **Unit Boss Test** (Lesson 7, like U10L7) | end of every unit | ~6 min inside a 20–30 min lesson | did this unit stick? | stars per skill + words to practise; a skill under 80 % → a "Practise again" homework pack |
| **Checkpoint 1 "Halfway Explorer"** | after Unit 5 | 15–20 min, its own lesson | is the foundation solid enough for Units 6–10? | Go on / Go on with support / Review first |
| **Final Test "Explorer Graduation"** | after Unit 10 | 20–25 min, its own lesson | ready for A1? | Ready for A1 / Almost ready / Not yet → feeds the level change request |

## 3. How each test is built (same rules for all)

- **One skill per section, 4 sections**, like the Starters shields:
  1. **Listening — words** (find the picture you hear: "Find the purple triangle!", "Find: grandma!")
  2. **Listening — sentences** (understand a short sentence: "The ball is under the bed.", "Can a fish walk?", "She can dance! Who is it?")
  3. **Sounds and early reading** (first sound of a word, then blending simple words — our Pre-A1 reading goal; no spelling)
  4. **Speaking** — rated live by the teacher with a 4-point rubric (0 no answer · 1 one word with help · 2 one word alone ·
     3 short phrase/sentence: "I can jump!", "It's a red ball.") on 4–5 prompts: point and name in a big picture, answer about
     object cards, personal questions (name, age, family, likes), "What can you do?".
- **One try per question** (it is a test), spoken prompt with 🔊 replay as often as needed, picture or letter answers only,
  no clock, no lives. A miss shows and says the right answer kindly, then moves on.
- **Items come only from what was taught** (each item lists the lesson it comes from), mixed across units, with distractors
  from the same topic.
- **Result per section on a 1–5 star scale** (like shields): 90–100 % = 5, 80–89 % = 4, 65–79 % = 3, 50–64 % = 2, under 50 % = 1.
  Speaking: average rubric × 5 / 3, rounded.
- The child always ends on a celebration (medal / certificate). The decision is shown to the **teacher and parent**, never as
  "failed" to the child.

## 4. The two big tests in detail

### Checkpoint 1 — "Halfway Explorer" (after Unit 5)
- Listening — words (8): greetings & feelings, colours & shapes, toys, body & face, family.
- Listening — sentences (6): "How old are you?" → age card; "The ball is under the chair"; "Touch your nose!"; "This is my
  grandpa"; "I am happy"; big / small.
- Sounds (6): first sounds taught in Units 1–5 (H M N W A S B T …) + 2 blends (e.g. *hat*, *mat*).
- Speaking (4 prompts): name and age; name 3 things in a picture (colour + object); "Who is this?" (family photo);
  "Show me your…/ What's this?" (body part).
- **Decision:**
  - **Go on** — at least 3 stars in every section and 13+ stars of 20 in all.
  - **Go on with support** — one section at 2 stars (or 11–12 in all): continue to Unit 6, and that skill's "Practise again"
    pack goes into homework for the next 2 weeks; the teacher re-checks it in the Unit 6 Boss Test.
  - **Review first** — two or more sections at 2 stars or less (or under 11 in all): 2 review lessons on the weak units,
    then a short re-check (the missed items in a new order with new pictures).

### Final Test — "Explorer Graduation" (after Unit 10, Pre-A1 → A1)
- Listening — words (10): all ten units, weighted to Units 6–10 (house, animals, food, clothes, actions).
- Listening — sentences (8): "I like apples", "He is wearing a hat", "Where is the cat? In the kitchen", "Can a bird fly?",
  "She can swim", colours + clothes, prepositions, numbers 1–10.
- Sounds & early reading (8): first sounds across the level + 3–4 CVC blends (pig, jam, log, cat).
- Speaking (5 prompts, Starters-style): describe a big picture (point and name), object cards ("What's this? What colour?"),
  personal questions (name, age, family, favourite food), "What can you do?", one story picture ("What is Pip doing?").
- **Decision (needs the teacher's confirmation, then the level change request goes to the admin as today):**
  - **Ready for A1** — every section 3+ stars, Listening sections and Speaking 4+, and 16+ of 20 stars in all.
  - **Almost ready** — every section 3+ stars but under 16 in all, or one section at 2: a 2–3 lesson "bridge" (the weak
    skill + A1 Welcome Town Lesson 1 preview), then the missed part again.
  - **Not yet** — two or more sections at 2 or less: stay in Pre-A1 for a review cycle of the weakest units (Practise again
    packs + review lessons), re-test in 3–4 weeks.

## 5. What we need to build (after the owner says yes)

1. A **Test scene** (Pre-A1): the Hero Trials engine from U10L7 extended with sections, star bands and a teacher-only
   speaking rubric panel; the student never sees a score word, only the celebration.
2. **Save the result** (new table `playground_assessment_results`: student, test id, section scores, item answers, rubric,
   decision, teacher confirmation) — so the decision survives the lesson and the teacher and parent can see it.
3. **Hook the Final Test into `level_change_requests`**: "Ready for A1" pre-fills a request (from Pre-A1 to A1, start Unit 1)
   with the section stars as `unit_marks`; the teacher confirms, the admin approves — the existing path, nothing bypassed.
4. **Boss Tests for Units 1–9** (Lesson 7 of each, like U10L7) — can follow gradually.
5. **Practise-again packs**: per skill and unit, made from existing homework-quest levels.
6. A teacher **result card** in the lesson and a short **parent report** (translated into the 6 languages).

Order: Final Test first (it decides the move to A1), then Checkpoint 1, then the unit Boss Tests for Units 1–9.
