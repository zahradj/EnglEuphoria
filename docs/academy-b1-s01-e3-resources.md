## 1 Pattern Card (student)

**Have you ever …? — the pattern**

1. **have / has + participle** = something you did **at some time up to now** (no time is said): *I have flown. She has eaten sushi.*
2. **never** = not once: *I have never been to Rome.* **ever** = at any time, in questions: *Have you ever flown in a plane? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.*
3. **been** = went and came back: *I've been to Lisbon.* **gone** = went and is still there: *Theo has gone to the library.*
**When? Where? Who with?** → say the details in the **past simple**: *I went last summer. I flew with Ava.*

| Say this (experience, up to now) | Not this |
|---|---|
| I have been to Rome twice. | ~~I have been to Rome last year.~~ (a finished time → *I went to Rome last year.*) |
| Have you ever eaten sushi? | ~~Did you ever eat sushi?~~ |
| She has gone to the library. (she is not here) | — |
| He has been to the library. (he is back) | — |

Participles to remember (from lesson 1): *been, gone, seen, eaten, taken, flown.*

## 2 Case File — evidence cards ("The Missing Postcard")

**Detective Board — sort these eight cards: FINISHED TIME (when) or UP TO NOW (experience)?**

| # | Card | Goes in | Why (teacher) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I have been to Rome twice. | Up to now | have + participle, no time |
| 2 | I went to Lisbon last summer. | Finished time | *last summer* = finished |
| 3 | Have you ever flown in a plane? | Up to now | *ever* |
| 4 | Ava flew home in July. | Finished time | *in July* |
| 5 | She has never eaten sushi. | Up to now | *never* |
| 6 | They took the bus yesterday. | Finished time | *yesterday* |
| 7 | We have seen the sea. | Up to now | have + participle |
| 8 | He ate sushi in 2023. | Finished time | *in 2023* |

**Statements (the story, and the Alibi):**
- **Ava:** "I've never been to Lisbon. I've been to Rome twice."
- **Mia:** "I've never taken a postcard from the wall. I have never been to Portugal."
- **Theo:** "I've never been to Lisbon." → asked "When did you go?" → "I went last summer." (**contradiction**: the student catches it)
- **Vee (the case):** "The Lisbon postcard is gone from my wall. Has anyone been near it?"

**Evidence Builder blocks** (build each sentence; answers in the teacher guide): `have / been / I / never / to / Rome` · `you / ever / Have / flown / ?` · `has / She / eaten / never / sushi` · `Theo / gone / has / to / the / library` · `Yes / I / have` · `No / I / haven't` · `When / you / did / go / ?` · `I / seen / have / the / sea`.

## 3 Been or Gone? — scene cards

Each card is a short picture + line; the student chooses **been** or **gone** and says why.

| # | Scene (picture) | Sentence | Answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Theo is back at his desk with a library book. | Theo has ___ to the library. | **been** | he came back |
| 2 | An empty desk, Theo's bag is gone. | Theo has ___ to the library. | **gone** | he is still there |
| 3 | A girl at a map, pointing at Paris. | Have you ever ___ to Paris? | **been** | ever → been |
| 4 | Mia's seat is empty, her coat is on the hook. | Mia has ___ to the canteen. | **gone** | not here now |
| 5 | Ava, tanned, with a suitcase at the door. | Ava has just ___ to Rome (she is home). | **been** | back |
| 6 | Vee's phone: "Back in 10 min!" | Vee has ___ out for ten minutes. | **gone** | still out |
| 7 | A photo of the whole class at the sea. | We have ___ to the sea twice. | **been** | a past experience |
| 8 | A "closed" sign on the library door. | The librarian has ___ home. | **gone** | not here |

## 4 Question Forge frames (Chill track and Take 1 support)

`Have you ever + participle + ?` — `Yes, I have. / No, I haven't. / No, never.`
Follow-up frames: `When did you + verb?` · `Where did you go?` · `Who did you go with?` · `What did you eat / see?`
Alibi: `Have you ever been to ___?` → `When did you go?` → `Who did you go with?`

## 5 My Five Sentences (Release sheet)

Write 3-5 sentences about **you**. The model is hidden in the lesson.
1. One with **never**: *I have never ____.*
2. One with **been**: *I have been to ____.*
3. One question with **ever**: *Have you ever ____?*
4. One past simple follow-up with a time: *I ____ it ____ (when).*
5. (Push) One with **gone** or a second experience.
Then **say** your sentences aloud (record a voice note if you like; it is private until you send it).
Self-check: have / has + participle? no finished time with *have*? *been* or *gone*?

## 6 Homework (Daily 10, no new work, only practice)

Daily 10 (10 minutes, on days 1, 3, 7, 14, 30): five questions from this lesson (build, been-or-gone, ever-questions) + the three words from your error list + one voice note (30 seconds): "Three things I have done and one thing I have never done." No ranking, no timer, retry allowed.

## 7 Teacher guide

**Before:** open the Case File; check `recallPlan` for the Remember? items (lesson-1 participles, lesson-2 words, the student's error log).
**Script, Alibi (Take 1):** you are Theo. Student: "Have you ever been to Lisbon?" → **Theo:** "No, never." → Student: "Have you ever been to Portugal?" → "Hmm … I've been once." → Student: "When did you go?" → "I went last summer." → Student: "You said never!" (**contradiction found**). Take 2: you are Mia; Mia's story is **consistent** (the student must see that too: not every suspect lies). The postcard is found: Theo took it to copy the stamp.
**Wait and correct:** wait 5-7 s; correct after the Mission, not during; one correction at a time.
**Answer key, Evidence Builder:** I have never been to Rome. · Have you ever flown? · She has never eaten sushi. · Theo has gone to the library. · Yes, I have. · No, I haven't. · When did you go? · I have seen the sea. (Accept the other order where it is correct: *I have never been to Rome* / *Never have I …* is **not** taught.)
**Answer key, sort:** finished time = 2, 4, 6, 8; up to now = 1, 3, 5, 7. Been or Gone: 1 been · 2 gone · 3 been · 4 gone · 5 been · 6 gone · 7 been · 8 gone.
**Error log (log up to 3):** a finished time with *have*; "Did you ever…?"; *gone* for *been* with *ever*; missing *have* / *has*; wrong participle; *ever* before the subject. Home-language slips are **possible**, not assumed: ask the student how it works in their language (see the research file, L1 point [U]).
**Checklist (1-5 "I can …"):** see plan §1; mark Nailed it / Almost / Needs more practice, the speaking step reached (shadow → repeat → controlled → guided → free), up to three recurring errors and comfort.
