## 1 The story
**The Bucket List Gazette.** The school paper is about to print its feature "Five bucket lists": the Season's first dare (who has done the most?) finally gets an answer in print, and nobody has checked it. Vee, the editor, hands the student the **fact-checker's desk** and Ava, the reporter, who wrote it, asks questions. The article (about 260 words, B1) lists what five club members have done and still want to do, with dates, numbers and two traps (a finished time with the wrong tense, and a number that does not add up). Theo's list from last lesson is one of the five. The student finds who has done the most, proves it with the lines, catches the slip, and relays the key points to Ava before the page goes to print. Stakes are tiny and warm: a correction, not a scandal.
Handed to E6 (My kind of adventure): the student's own list.

## 2 The game concept: "Fact-Check Desk"
1. **Headline match** (skim): match a headline to each paragraph (gist first, as in the Cambridge guide).
2. **Archive Search** (signature reading game, new scene `archive`): the whole article sits in a newsroom archive. The student **taps keyword chips** (Italy, twice, last summer, in total…) and the desk returns at most **three passages** per search, as short clippings; new keywords appear inside the clippings (Her Story). Facts are pinned to the **Editor's Board** (name, what they have done, how many, when). No typing, no timer, no score bar.
3. **Fact-Check Wall** (judge): True / False / Not given cards, each with the line that proves it; answers are confirmed in **threes** (Obra Dinn) so guessing does not pay; a wrong card reads as "no line says that", never a buzzer.
4. **Relay** (Mission, spoken): the student tells Ava the key points with frames (*According to the article… / In total… / So far…*), Take 1 guided, Take 2 without frames; Ava asks two follow-ups (*Who has done the most? When did she go?*).
5. **Red Pen** (Release, written): write a five-sentence note for the Gazette; three of the student's sentences are flagged by the pen (tense, participle, finished time), the student rewrites them (a self-edit with a target, as in the editing research).
Reward: the board stamps **CHECKED**, the Gazette page "prints" with the student's correction; the highlight reel returns as the pull quote.

## 3 Student, objective, criteria
Same student (B1, one-to-one, ar/es/fr/tr/it). From E1-E4: the present perfect pattern, past simple follow-ups, host chunks. **Need now:** to read a longer text for details and report it, instead of reading word by word and giving up at the first unknown word; and to keep *have + participle* vs the past simple straight in writing.
**Objective:** I can read a longer text about experiences, find the key details, decide what the text says (true, false, not given), tell the key points to someone else, and write a short note and fix my own mistakes.
**Exit task (Release):** a five-sentence note with a rewrite of three corrected sentences.
**Success criteria:**
1. I can skim a longer text and say what it is mainly about.
2. I can find a key detail (who, how many, when) by scanning.
3. I can decide if a statement is true, false or not given, and show the line.
4. I can relay the key points with *have / has* and the past simple (According to the article, Ava has been to Rome twice. She went in May.).
5. I can write a short note and rewrite it to fix three mistakes.
**New items (receptive, ≤ 8):** according to · in total · at least · more than · so far · still to do · altogether · the first / the latest. No new grammar.
