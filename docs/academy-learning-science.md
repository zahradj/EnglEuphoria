# Academy learning science layer — what we do, why, how sure we are, how we observe it

Companion to `docs/academy-lesson-system.md` (Seasons, run of show, roadmap A1 → C1). Evidence base: `docs/research/academy-lesson-design.md` plus the verification pass of 2026-10-08.

**Reading the labels.** **[V]** = finding seen in a search result this session (mostly abstracts or secondary summaries — check against the paper before public use) · **[K]** = established literature known from memory, not re-checked · **[RT]** = rule of thumb, no direct study · **[U]** = unverified.
**The honest headline:** most pooled evidence is lab-based, university-age or from general (non-language) learning. Almost none of it was tested on *teenagers in a digital one-to-one player*. So every technique below is a **well-supported hypothesis we will measure** (§5), not a proven result. We will not claim otherwise in marketing.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Learning ≠ performance.** What a student gets right *during* the session is a poor index of what they will retain (Soderstrom & Bjork 2015) [V]. So we never treat in-session score, XP or streak as proof of learning; progress is judged on **delayed, production-based evidence** (§4).
2. **Retrieve, don't re-read; space, don't mass.** Both are the best-supported effects we have; we build them into the session and between sessions.
3. **Notice, then check the rule.** Discovery is a route into the rule, not a replacement for stating it.
4. **Output must be pushed, then repaired.** Students produce, get specific feedback, and try again.
5. **Teach chunks and words in several skills, deliberately.** Incidental learning alone is too weak.
6. **Perception before production for sounds**, with many voices.
7. **Reduce anxiety so students will speak** — it is a measurable drag on achievement.
8. **Build self-regulation** (goals, strategies, reflection) as part of the lesson, not an extra.

## 2. Technique → mechanism in the system

| 1 | **Retrieval practice** | Testing vs restudy g ≈ 0.50 (Rowland 2014, 61 mostly-lab studies); classroom synthesis g ≈ 0.50 (Yang 2021, secondary source); bigger with feedback and delays ≥ 1 day | [V] numbers via secondary summaries; effect is *general*, not L2-specific | Remember?, Daily 10, Remix, checkpoint | Production first (type/say with the answer hidden), recognition second; feedback after every item; ≥ 60 % of review items are production [RT] |
| 2 | **Spaced repetition** | Spacing beats massing; longer gaps win on *delayed* tests; expanding ≈ equal schedules (L2 meta-analysis summarised in a thesis: 98 effect sizes, 48 experiments) | [V] thesis abstract; most data are flashcard vocabulary | `srs.ts`, Daily 10, Remember? | Items scheduled by **calendar days since seen**: same session → +1 → +3 → +7 → +21 → +60; a miss drops to +1 [RT for the exact numbers]. ## 3. Corrections this research forced on our earlier docs

- ❌ "Interleave: E7 mixes Seasons" as *interleaving of vocabulary*. ✅ Mixed review stays (spacing/retrieval), but new words are learned in blocks; only confusable grammar contrasts get interleaved. Updated in `academy-lesson-system.md` §2.2.
- ❌ Treating the 95–98 % coverage as a threshold. ✅ A guide ("about"), flexible per student.
- ❌ Shadowing as pronunciation fix. ✅ Prosody supplement; perception training with multiple voices is the supported route.
- ❌ Assuming prompts always beat recasts, or that written correction works the same at every level. ✅ Feedback ladder by level and mode (§2 row 8).
- ❌ Implying 4/3/2 fluency repetition is proven. ✅ Evidence is mixed (accuracy/fluency up, complexity can fall); we measure per student.
- ❌ In-session XP/stars as progress. ✅ Progress = delayed production evidence (§4).

## 4. How we observe that the student has *acquired* the knowledge

### 4.1 Knowledge items (the unit of observation)
Every word, chunk and structure is an **item** with: first seen · encounters by skill (read, heard, said, written) · retrieval history (date, mode, correct/incorrect, hints used) · current interval · state (**New → Learning → Known → Secure → Fading**) · last production sample.

- **Known** = correct production on ≥ 2 occasions on different days.
- **Secure** = correct production after a gap ≥ 21 days *and* used in a freer task (not only a drill).
- **Fading** = overdue or recently missed. States can fall.

### 4.2 Evidence the student, teacher and parent see
- **Per skill** (listening, reading, speaking, writing, plus vocabulary and grammar): Attempted → Familiar → Proficient → **Mastered only after a later mixed check**.
- **Retention score** each Remember?: "kept 7 of 9".
- **Delayed retest** items inside the checkpoint (not the practice items).
- **Production samples:** one speaking and one writing sample at the start and end of every Season, scored with a CEFR-aligned rubric (accuracy, range, fluency, coherence, interaction) and kept as the before/after portfolio.
- **Training-Wheels meter:** hints per session trending down.
- **Level claims** (A2, B1…) come only from a **separate placement/level test**, retaken every 2 Seasons [RT] and at each level boundary; never from XP.

### 4.3 What we deliberately do *not* use as evidence
Streak length, XP, same-session accuracy, time on task.

## 5. How we will test whether it works (pilot)

- **Design:** 5–10 students, one A1 Season (8 sessions), ages split 11–13 / 14–18, a mix of 1, 2 and 3 sessions a week.
- **Measures:** delayed retention (items at +7 and +21 days) · hints per session · talk-time share (`TalkTimeMeter`) · before/after speaking and writing rubric scores · enjoyment emoji · Daily-10 completion · teacher notes.
- **Within-student comparisons (small N):** for one technique at a time (e.g. Take 2 with vs without planning time; shadowing vs none), alternate across Seasons and compare delayed scores. Small samples → report patterns, not statistics.
- **Decide** from the data: keep, change or drop each technique; update this document.

## 6. Teacher protocol (for one-to-one)

- **Wait time:** 5–7 s before helping; then nudge, don't tell.
- **Talk time:** student ≥ 60 % at A1, ≥ 70 % at B1+, ≥ 75 % at C1.
- **Feedback ladder:** nudge → clue → direct; delayed correction during fluency tasks; always end an error on a success (retry until right).
- **Error log:** teacher taps errors into the Mistake Bank (item + type); the Daily 10 and next Remember? use them.
- **Never:** correct mid-sentence in a Take 1, rank the student against others, or force camera/voice.

## 7. Claims we will **not** make publicly

- That any single technique is proven for teenage L2 learners in a digital one-to-one player.
- That 95 % or 98 % coverage is a hard threshold.
- That prompts definitely beat recasts, or that written correction works at all levels.
- That interleaving helps vocabulary; that shadowing fixes sounds.
- That extensive-reading gains for teens equal adults'.
- That goal-setting/self-assessment raise scores (not verified here).
- Any number marked "secondary" until checked against the abstract.
- Any claim that the course delivers a CEFR level in a set time: guided hours are guidelines (Cambridge says so).
