# A1 Unit 1 · Welcome Town — new unit plan and lesson plans

Status: **draft for the owner's approval** (2026-10-12). The seven lessons are rebuilt from this plan only after the owner
says yes. Owner's request: *"some activities don't make sense — Who said it? doesn't make sense, it's not relevant … create
the unit plan and the lesson plans again … enjoyable, attractive and easy to understand … the purpose and objective of each
lesson clear, every activity serves the objective and the student's need … in A1, not just CVC words: they need to learn to
read full sentences, so focus on the story … blending games … quick reading like tongue twisters, easy at the beginning and
harder as we go to the last unit."*

## 1. What is wrong with Unit 1 today (audit of the built lessons)

| Problem | Where | Why it fails the child |
|---|---|---|
| **"Who said it?" games** — hear a line, tap the cartoon who said it | L3 *Who sings it?*, L4 *Who says it?*, L5 *Who said it?*, L6 *Buzzer: who says the line* + *Who is it?*, L7 Level 1, and the Remember? warm-ups of L4, L5, L7 | Knowing **which cartoon** said "How are you?" is not a skill anyone needs. The child must understand and **use** the greeting, not identify a voice. 8 pages in the unit train nothing from the objective. |
| **"Any answer is right" pages** (*my favourite word / line / game*) | every lesson, often twice | No language goal, no decision. Filler. |
| **CVC blending with unrelated words** — hat, pig, sun, dog, sad | L1-L5 *Blend It!* | The words have nothing to do with greetings or the story, and the blend ends at one word. A1 children need to **read sentences**. |
| **No reading strand** | whole unit | Only L2 (*Pip's Tired Day*) and L5 have a short story. The child never reads a growing story; reading is word-level. |
| **The same games again and again** | Memory in L1, L2, L3, L4, L6 · Spin & Say in L1-L4, L6 · Quick Fire in L1-L4, L6 · the same "sing-back" page in L1-L4 | Boring, and breaks the rule "no game twice in a unit". |
| **L3's objective is "say who sings each line"** | L3 plan + lesson | The objective itself is the irrelevant skill. |
| **Plans and lessons disagree** | `a1LessonPlans.ts` Unit 1 | e.g. the L4 plan says *This is my friend… / Nice to meet you*; the lesson barely uses it. |
| L6 has 15 pages, L7 13 | L6, L7 | Too short for 30 minutes. |

## 2. Unit objective

**By the end of Unit 1 the child can meet someone new in English — say hello, say and ask names, ask and answer
"How are you?", introduce a friend and say goodbye — and READ ALOUD the short story *Leo's First Day* (11 pages, about
25 sentences built from those same lines).**

> *"Hello! My name is Pip. What's your name?" — "Hi, Pip! My name is Leo." — "How are you, Leo?" — "I am sad. My red cap
> is lost." — "Mia, this is my friend Leo." — "Nice to meet you!" … "Goodbye, friends!"*

Evidence: the Boss Test (L7) — listening, **reading the story's sentences** (yes/no about a picture and fill-the-gap, the
Cambridge Starters formats), a 6-line conversation with the teacher, and reading one story page aloud. 80 % in every part.

## 4. The reading strand — *Leo's First Day* (one story, read a bit more each lesson)

Leo is new in Welcome Town and he has lost his red cap. Pip says hello, the friends meet him, and together they find the
cap. Every lesson reads its episode (read-along first, then the child reads); L5 reads the whole book.

| Page | Line (what the child reads) | Read first in |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | This is Leo. Leo is new in Welcome Town. | L1 |
| 2 | **Pip:** "Hello! My name is Pip. What's your name?" | L1 |
| 3 | **Leo:** "Hi, Pip! My name is Leo." | L1 |
| 4 | **Pip:** "How are you, Leo?" **Leo:** "I am sad. My red cap is lost." | L2 |
| 5 | **Pip:** "Come on, Leo! Let's find it!" | L2 |
| 6 | **Pip:** "Mia, this is my friend Leo." **Mia:** "Hello, Leo! Nice to meet you." | L4 |
| 7 | **Bella:** "Hi, Leo! I'm Bella. Look! A red cap!" | L4 |
| 8 | **Willow:** "No, no! That is my red hat!" *(the surprise)* | L4 |
| 9 | **Mia:** "Look! The cap is on the cat!" | L5 |
| 10 | **Leo:** "My cap! Thank you, friends! I am happy now!" | L5 |
| 11 | **All:** "Goodbye, Leo! See you tomorrow!" **Leo:** "Goodbye, friends!" | L5 |

Pictures: 11 story pictures in Canva (cut-out cast on the Welcome Town backgrounds we already have; one base picture
per place, so the characters stay the same). Boys (Pip, Leo) never in a dress. L5 gets a **stills film** of the book (scenario first, cross-fades, no zoom, read-along
subtitles, recorded voices) — no paid clips; cost: Canva only.

## 5. Lesson by lesson

Every lesson keeps the A1 blueprint frame (§3g of `docs/playground-lesson-blueprint.md`): title → Remember? → the new
language → a first game → the song → more practice → the reader → Blend & Read → tongue twister → the child's own
conversation (free practice) → sticker + passport → home mission → goodbye song. **No game is used twice in the unit**
(table §5.8). Every page below says **why it is there** — if a page cannot say which part of the objective it trains, it
is not in the lesson.

Stages: **P** present · **G** guided practice · **F** free practice · **R** reading · **✓** check.

## 6. The A1 tongue-twister ladder (easy in Unit 1 → hard in Unit 10)

Every A1 lesson has one twister after Blend & Read; it uses the lesson's sound. The ladder grows in **length** (3 → 10
words), **sound mix** (one sound → two → contrasting sounds) and **speed** (Units 1-3: slow + normal; Units 4-7: + fast;
Units 8-10: 3× fast, beat your own time).

| Unit | Level | Example (the unit's sound) | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Welcome Town | 1 — one sound, 3-4 words | *Happy Hen hops.* · *Sad Sam sits.* · *Fun Fred finds a frog.* | 3-5 |
| 2 Jungle | 2 — one sound, longer | *Chip the chimp chews chips.* · *A duck on a rock eats a snack.* | 5-7 |
| 3 Space | 2 — one sound, repeated twice | *Three thin things sing a song.* · *Ring-a-ding, sing-a-song!* | 6 |
| 4 Food Festival | 3 — two blends | *Brown bread, green grapes, plum snacks!* | 6 |
| 5 Monster Town | 3 — vowel team, 3× | *Green sheep eat sweet green peas.* | 6 |

| 8 Dino Valley | 4 — oo / ar mixed | *A large dark shark eats food at noon.* | 8 |
