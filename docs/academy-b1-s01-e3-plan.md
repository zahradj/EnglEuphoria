## 1 Student and objective
One teen (13-18) at B1, one-to-one, home language ar / es / fr / tr / it. From lesson 1 the student has met "Have you ever …? / I have never …" and the participles (flown, eaten, gone, taken, been, seen) and sorted "when" sentences from "up to now" sentences **without a rule**; from lesson 2 they have 14 words for feelings, places and activities. **Need now:** to *say the pattern correctly and choose it*. Today they use the past simple for everything ("Did you ever go to Rome?") or add a finished time to the present perfect ("I have been to Rome last year").
**Hard for them (L1, `l1Watch` B1-S01; the L1 point is [U], see the research file):** *been* vs *gone*; a finished time with the present perfect; `have` forgotten or `has` for he/she; the participle form (wrong for irregular verbs); *ever* in the wrong place.
**Objective (observable):** By the end I can ask and answer "Have you ever …?" and say "I have / I have never …" with the right participle, use *been* and *gone* correctly, and follow with a past simple question or sentence (when / where) — in a question-and-answer round and in five sentences of my own.
**Exit task (the Release):** write 3-5 sentences of my own with the pattern (at least one *never*, one *been*, one past simple follow-up with a time), then say them aloud; model hidden.
**Success criteria (teacher marks in the checklist, 1-5 "I can …"):**
1. I can build a correct "I have / I have never + participle" sentence from the parts.
2. I can ask "Have you ever + participle …?" and answer "Yes, I have. / No, I haven't."
3. I can choose *been* or *gone* for a short scene and say why.
4. I can tell an "up to now" sentence from a "finished time" sentence and follow an experience with a past simple question or sentence (When did you …?).
5. I can write and say five sentences of my own about my experiences with the pattern.
**New items (chunks, ≤ 8, the Season has one structure):** I have + participle · I have never + participle · Have you ever + participle …? · Yes, I have. / No, I haven't. · He / She has been · He / She has gone · I've … once / twice · When did you …? (the past simple follow-up; the verb forms are known). No new vocabulary words: the lesson reuses lesson 1 and lesson 2 words (≥ 95 % known in every text).

## 2 Look, story frame and variety (vs lessons 1 and 2)
Lesson 1: bright Club classroom with a corkboard; lesson 2: the Travel Club room with the postcard wall (the made pictures; locker-hall corridor for the Release). ## 4 Speaking (B1 target: student talk ≥ 55 %, ≥ 20 speaking minutes, ≥ 5 speaking tasks, reaches the *free* step)
Speaking minutes ≈ 24: Never Have I Ever 2, repeating the rule 1, saying every build / sort / question answer aloud 8, the Energiser 3, Alibi Take 1 + Take 2 + feedback 7, the spoken Release 2, teach-back 1. Ladder: shadow (say after the guide) → repeat → controlled (build and ask) → guided (Alibi frames, Chill) → free (Alibi Normal / Push, Release). Voice notes between lessons: 3 a week (Daily 10).

## 5 Practice ladder, interleaving and the error loop
Guided (model, rule, builder) → semi-independent (sort, question forge) → independent (Alibi Normal) → mixed (Never Have I Ever and the Energiser mix lesson-1 participles with lesson-2 words and the new pattern). **Error loop:** misses in the builder and the sort return in the next set; the three logged errors return in the next Remember? and the Daily 10 (days 1, 3, 7, 14, 30).

## 6 Games, video-game mechanic and the signature game
From the **171-game catalogue** (by number): **169** Never Have I Ever, **19** Grammar Detective: Rule Discovery, **17** Sentence Builder Blocks, **61** Alibi (signature game), **117** Detective Case File (the frame), plus the vocabulary-menu drag-match for recall. **New finds (not in the catalogue yet):** Been or Gone? case sort, Evidence Builder (a themed Sentence Builder), Question forge. **Video-game mechanic: Detective board + deduction log** (*Return of the Obra Dinn*): pin evidence, fill a who / has been where / when log; what the student says in English: the question and the follow-up that expose the contradiction. No game three in a row; at least five different games; Glitch Hunt, Trophy Forge and Mood Postcards are not repeated.

## 9 Supports, assessment and follow-up
Supports: home-language hint on request; slower audio; Chill track (Alibi frames); Stretch (Push dial); type instead of speak. Comfort: private before public, no timers on speaking, no ranking, "Not yet – try again"; the "accused" is only a puzzle character, never a real person; the postcard is found at the end. Assessment: the five criteria in the teacher checklist; production-weighted evidence from the Alibi and the Release; the delayed check in the next Remember?. Follow-up: Daily 10 days 1, 3, 7, 14, 30; voice notes; the next clue unlocks "building a correct sentence with the pattern". **Teacher notes:** wait 5-7 s; correct after the Mission, not during; log up to 3 errors; listen for a finished time with the present perfect ("I have been there last year"), "Did you ever…?", *gone* for *been* in questions, a missing *have* / *has*.
