# Pre-A1 Unit 1 Lesson 5 — "Leo's Lost Star" (story lesson) — rebuild plan

**Status:** owner request 2026-10-13 ("recreate it — do research, create a lesson plan, and refactor it; PPP or any
sequence that helps the student understand; use the games") — built the same day.

## What was wrong with the old version (28 pages)

- **The story was cut in half.** Part 1 of the film came at page 5, part 2 only at page 19, after 13 games. The child
  played the search game ("Is it under the hat?") *before* seeing the search in the film.
- **The new words came after the film**, so the first watch was hard to follow.
- **Games borrowed from other lessons** (memory, dash, sound-sort, quick-fire, hello-doors, who-said-it are already in
  Lessons 1-4 and 6). The blueprint gives the story slot its own games, and no game should come twice in a unit.
- **Speaking was scattered**: the "Leo asks you / you ask Leo" pages sat in the middle, so the lesson did not build up
  to the child talking.

## The sequence: Pre → While → Post (the story version of Present → Practice → Produce)

For a story lesson the research favours **pre-, while- and post-story stages**: picture walk and key words with
pictures *before* (present), watching with pauses, gestures and predictions *during*, and retelling, acting and
checking *after* (practice → produce). It is PPP with the story in the middle.

## The new game: Wind Catch (`wind-catch`)

- **What happens:** a windy meadow; the story things (hat, bat, mat, ant, nut, star…) float across the sky on the wind,
  bobbing, at different heights. Leo says "Catch the bat!". The child taps it: it drops into Leo's basket (it stays),
  "A bat! /b/, bat!". A wrong one is named back ("That's the hat!") and blows on. The last round is "Catch my star!" and
  Leo is happy.
- **Research (mechanics only):** catch-the-falling-object arcade games of kids' apps (Lingokids, Khan Academy Kids
  "catch" play), Cambridge Pre A1 "listen and point", and the story's own wind beat.
- **Better than them:** nothing is timed or lost — the things keep floating until the child is ready; every catch is
  named back with its first sound (the unit's phonics); the basket keeps what was caught; the game *is* the story
  (the wind took the star; we catch it back).

## Research

- Pre/while/post story stages, picture walk and prediction, pauses for repeated phrases and actions, then retelling
  (Quebec MEES "Picture Walk"; JALT *The Language Teacher* "Happily ever after: post-reading projects with picturebooks";
  practitioner lesson plans; Cambridge and British Council storytelling guides used in the first version).
- Total Physical Response Storytelling: story + body movement + repetition works with 4-5-year-olds (Universidad de
  Cuenca TPRS study, 18 sessions, preschool).
- Kids' apps: interactive read-along books with characters as tap hot spots (Khan Academy Kids); fast short games and
  songs (Lingokids).
