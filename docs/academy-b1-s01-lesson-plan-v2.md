## 1 Student and objective
One teen (13–18) at B1, one-to-one. Knows the past simple and many base forms, mixes up "when it happened" (past simple) and "up to now" (present perfect), and says *I have went / I have been to Italy last year*. L1 transfer (ar, fr, it, tr): the compound past is used for finished events, so a stated time is the main trap. Comfort: low-pressure first, nothing public.

**Objective (can-do, observable):** By the end the student can talk about their own life experiences for about a minute — say what they have (never) done, give one or two details in the past simple, and ask a friend "Have you ever…?" with a follow-up question — without reading.
**Exit task (built backwards):** a one-minute spoken account recorded privately (three things done, one with a short story in the past simple, one thing they want to do), plus three written sentences.
**Success criteria (teacher marks each):** 1 I follow the main idea and detail of a short blog post and the dialogue. 2 I understood the six new words. 3 I say the three forms of nine verbs. 4 I choose past simple vs present perfect and write a question and an answer. 5 I speak for about a minute with a reason and a follow-up.

## 2 Method (why this shape)
One cycle of **modelling → clarification → controlled practice → guided practice → free practice → production**, wrapped in Nation's four strands (input, language-focused study, output, fluency) so that every skill is used, and in Scrivener's ARC (authentic use first and last, restricted use in the middle, clarification between). The teacher models; the student takes over a little more on each page (gradual release); the grammar is noticed in a real text before the rule is stated (guided discovery, as in the British Council and teacher plans), then drilled in short games, then used in talk and writing. Errors are noted in one list and returned to at the end of free practice (delayed correction).

## 3b Answer to the owner's review (2026-10-15)
- **Comprehension questions in every reading and listening segment:** yes. The listening segment has 4 questions (audio only, transcript hidden until after), the reading segment has 4 (gist, detail, inference, word in context). The earlier build had them but with only 3 and no word-in-context; both go to 4.
- **Fill-in-the-gaps with the vocabulary learned:** yes. After the comprehension questions the student completes the blog post with the six new words and phrases (word bank, one retry, hint). Retrieval of words in context is stronger for vocabulary than matching alone, so it comes after the two matching games and before the grammar. A new page type (`cloze`) is needed.
- **A listening segment with a voice clip:** yes, a real one with recorded approved voices (Vee, Ava, Theo). The lines exist in the deck but are not baked yet (cost about 30 cents per 1,000 characters, audited first), so the segment is silent until you say "bake". Audio-only first play is new.
- **All four skills at once to serve grammar and vocabulary:** yes, and it is the right shape for a B1 teen. Listening and reading give the language in context (input), the cloze and the games fix the vocabulary and forms (language-focused), writing and speaking use them (output), and the Wordle and "Never have I ever" round build fluency. Each skill carries both targets: the texts are written so the six words and the present perfect/past simple contrast occur in them, the cloze recycles the words, the writing task needs both tenses, and the Mission needs both words and tenses in speech. The risk is length: the hour is full, so extras stay optional.

## 4 Games and video-game mechanics (named, each tied to a criterion)
Hot Seat (quiz ladder, lifelines) · The Dare (visual novel on three plates) · Grammar Detective (detective board) · Break the Clock (boss fight) · Triple Match (match-3 combos) · Participle Wordle (word puzzle) · Rapport (dialogue RPG) · Memory/Match (cards). Optional extras for a fast student: Trophy Forge (crafting), Verb Trio Memory, the speed streak.

## 5 Error loop, mastery and checklist
Error list: the teacher taps up to 3 recurring errors (tense after a time word, *have went*, missing *have*), they start the next Remember?. Mastery check: criterion 4 = the written question and answer correct first try plus Break the Clock without losing a shield; criterion 5 = Take 2 has a reason and a follow-up. Daily 10 (days 1, 3, 7, 14, 30): the nine verbs and the kept words; voice note between lessons.
