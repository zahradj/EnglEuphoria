Sources used (primary): Cambridge "Pre A1 Starters, A1 Movers and A2 Flyers Wordlists (for exams from 2018)" PDF, https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/pl/Images/149681-yle-flyers-word-list.pdf (I downloaded and parsed it locally); Cambridge Starters picture-book wordlist, https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/images/351849-pre-a1-starters-word-list-2018.pdf ; Cambridge YLE Handbook for Teachers (OLDER pre-2018 edition, but grammar lists still useful), https://assets.cambridgeenglish.org/network-services/yle-handbook-for-teachers.pdf

## 1. Official Cambridge Pre A1 Starters / A1 Movers / A2 Flyers vocabulary, topics, grammar scope; what "Pre A1" means

### Takeaway
Starters = about 480-500 listed words (incl. 20 names), Movers adds about 400 new, Flyers adds about 500 new, so cumulative about 1,400 (my own count, see caveats). Starters is listening/recognition-heavy, alphabet and numbers 1-20, present simple/continuous, can, there is/are, prepositions of place. Movers adds past simple, comparatives, must/have to; Flyers adds present perfect, going to/will, conditionals.

### Cited Findings
- The exams are "designed specifically for young learners aged 6-12", aligned to CEFR; "candidates need to be able to understand the words on the wordlist for their test" — [Cambridge wordlists 2018](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/pl/Images/149681-yle-flyers-word-list.pdf)
- Level mapping on the Cambridge scale: Pre A1 Starters, A1 Movers, A2 Flyers — same PDF.
- Starters wordlist is the base list; Movers and Flyers lists contain only NEW words (Flyers candidates need all lower lists too) — same PDF and [YLE handbook](https://assets.cambridgeenglish.org/network-services/yle-handbook-for-teachers.pdf) ("Flyers candidates will be expected to know everything on this list in addition to the lists at Starters and Movers").

### Inferences
- My own headword count by regex on the 2018 PDF (entries ending in a part-of-speech tag): Starters about 490, Movers about 400, Flyers about 510 new; cumulative about 1,400. The A-Z lists include names, function words, and some duplicates (e.g. polysemous entries), so treat as +/-10%. Concrete-noun picture-testable share at Starters is roughly 60-65 percent (my estimate).
- A placement test that only needs the Starters list (about 500) covers every plausible Pre-A1 content; A1 Movers adds past simple and question words, which a non-reading 4-9 year-old rarely needs.
- Combined CEFR vocabulary-size norms (see section 5) put 1,400 Cambridge words well under typical A2 "1,500-2,500 words" figure, which is consistent.

### Gaps
- Exact official word totals per level (the PDFs have no stated total); only my regex count and a secondary "over 500" claim.

## 2. Phonics and letter/sound knowledge expected at Pre-A1 vs A1; testing without a microphone

### Takeaway
Jolly Phonics order is s,a,t,i,p,n | c/k,e,h,r,m,d | g,o,u,l,f,b | ai,j,oa,ie,ee,or | z,w,ng,v,oo,oo | y,x,ch,sh,th,th | qu,ou,oi,ue,er,ar. Pre-A1: letter names plus the first ~6-12 sounds and a handful of tricky words; A1: all 26 letter sounds, CVC blending, ~40-100 sight words, short sentences. Sound-to-letter matching is testable with audio-only prompts and tap responses.

### Cited Findings
- Jolly Phonics sequence of 42 sounds in seven groups: 1 s,a,t,i,p,n; 2 c k,e,h,r,m,d; 3 g,o,u,l,f,b; 4 ai,j,oa,ie,ee,or; 5 z,w,ng,v,oo,oo; 6 y,x,ch,sh,th,th; 7 qu,ou,oi,ue,er,ar — [Jolly Phonics scope-and-sequence summary via search](https://www.stjuliansprimary.com/jolly-phonics) (secondary; consistent with Jolly Learning's own sequence)
- Letters and Sounds Phase 2 letters are taught in five sets: s,a,t,p | i,n,m,d | g,o,c,k | ck,e,u,r | h,b,f,ff,l,ll,ss; the Phase 2 "tricky/high-frequency" words are I, no, the, to, go, into — [Spellzone L&S Phase 2](https://www.spellzone.com/curriculum/letters-and-sounds/phase-2.cfm) and [Poolhouse school phase 2 page](https://poolhouse.lancs.sch.uk/our-curriculumn/phonics/phase-2) (search-snippet level).
- Dolch Pre-Primer (40 words): a, and, away, big, blue, can, come, down, find, for, funny, go, help, here, I, in, is, it, jump, little, look, make, me, my, not, one, play, red, run, said, see, the, three, to, two, up, we, where, yellow, you. Dolch Primer (52): all, am, are, at, ate, be, black, brown, but, came, did, do, eat, four, get, good, have, he, into, like, must, new, no, now, on, our, out, please, pretty, ran, ride, saw, say, she, so, soon, that, there, they, this, too, under, want, was, well, went, what, white, who, will, with, yes — [Wikipedia Dolch word list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolch_word_list) / [Wharton printable Dolch lists](https://wrdsclassroom.wharton.upenn.edu/dolch-word-lists-printable_35673.html). Dolch total list is 220 service words + 95 nouns.
- Cambridge Starters can-do: "recognise the letters of the English alphabet", "write the letters", "read short, simple words and the names of some objects", "recognise and copy words, phrases and short sentences" — [YLE handbook](https://assets.cambridgeenglish.org/network-services/yle-handbook-for-teachers.pdf)

### Inferences (flagged: my reasoning, not published thresholds)
- Suggested expectation bands (EFL child, no L1-English literacy assumed): Pre-A1 = recognise/name many of 26 letters (upper-case first), know ~6-12 sounds (Jolly group 1-2), recognise own name and 3-6 sight words (I, a, the, is, my, yes, no); A1 = 26 letter sounds, blend CVC (cat, sit, pen, dog, sun, bag, hop), read ~20-60 of Dolch pre-primer/primer words, read a 4-6 word sentence ("I see a cat", "It is big"); A2 = read 20-60-word texts, digraphs (sh, ch, th, ai, ee, oa), past forms.
- Mic-free phonics items: (a) "Which letter says /s/?" — hear isolated recorded sound, tap 1 of 3-4 letters (foils: visually/auditorily similar b/d, m/n, p/b, i/e); (b) "Which picture starts with /k/?" — tap picture (audio names all pictures; avoids reading); (c) hear a word, tap the letter it begins with; (d) CVC blend: hear /c/ /a/ /t/ segmented audio and tap picture or tap written word among 3 (cat/cut/cot — use minimal pairs for A1); (e) hear "the" / "is" and tap the matching written word among 3 (sight-word recognition); (f) read-and-tap: written word -> choose picture (decoding with no audio, tests true reading); (g) written sentence -> choose picture. Project rule (CLAUDE.md/memory): isolated phonics sounds must only play the recorded clip.
- Tricky for non-native children: /æ/ vs /e/ (cat/pen), /i/ vs /iː/, /θ/, and letter-name vs letter-sound confusion (child taps "ay" for /a/); English letter NAMES (e.g., "double-u", "aitch") are unfamiliar in many L1s.

### Gaps
- No published thresholds for how many letter sounds a Pre-A1 vs A1 EFL child should know; bands above are my judgement. Fry/Oxford Reading Tree first-100 lists were not pulled (Dolch used instead).
- Letters and Sounds and Jolly Phonics sources were secondary/snippet-level.

## 3. Graded listening-comprehension task types for non-readers (easiest -> hardest)

### Takeaway
Order items by (a) number of information elements the child must hold, (b) grammatical load (noun < verb < preposition < wh-question < story), and (c) distractor similarity. Cambridge Starters tasks themselves are 'picture + short dialogue' tasks (draw lines, tick one of three pictures, colour following instructions).

### Cited Findings
- Cambridge Starters Listening: Part 1 "carry out instructions and position things correctly on a picture"; Part 3 "3-option multiple-choice pictures and dialogues"; Part 4 "carry out instructions, locate objects, and colour correctly" using prepositions of place and ten colours; tasks are heard twice — [YLE handbook](https://assets.cambridgeenglish.org/network-services/yle-handbook-for-teachers.pdf)
- CEFR Pre-A1 oral comprehension: short very simple questions and statements, slowly and clearly, with visual support, repeated if necessary — [CEFR descriptors search page](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors-search) (snippet).
- Cambridge's own prep advice: Starters/Part 1 teachers recommend practising prepositions and colours; "Where is Monkey?" answers include between, next to, in, in front of, on, behind, under — [Starters picture book answers](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/images/351849-pre-a1-starters-word-list-2018.pdf).

### Inferences (proposed ladder; my design, not published norms)
| Level | Task | Example item (audio) | Response | Likely error pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Noun -> picture (3-4 choices) | "cat" / "Touch the apple" | tap | none for knowers; random for never-heard |
| L2 | Greeting/instruction, single action | "Stand up." "Clap your hands." "Point to your nose." | tap picture or on-screen action | confuses similar verbs (sit/stand) |
| L3 | Colour + noun | "Where is the red ball?" "What colour is the cat?" | tap | picks right noun/wrong colour (or reverse) |
| L4 | Number + noun | "Touch three apples." "How many dogs?" | tap picture with right count | counts 1-5 fine, 6-10 and teens (13/30, 14/40) confused |
| L5 | Verb + picture | "Who is eating?" "She is jumping." (present continuous) | tap | verb-noun swap; ignores -ing |
| L6 | Preposition scene | "Where is the cat? It's on the table." | tap | in/on, under/behind confused; in front of/next to later |
| L7 | Two attribute | "the big red ball", "Is it big or small?" | tap | picks one attribute only |
| L8 | Yes/no and either-or questions | "Is the dog big?" "Can the fish fly?" "Can you swim?" | yes/no buttons | acquiescence bias to "yes" (guessing = 50%) |
| L9 | wh- question matching | "Who is that?" "What does she like?" "How old is he?" | choose from 3 spoken/pictured answers | answers a different wh-word |
| L10 | Sequencing / short story (3-4 sentences) | "First the boy gets up, then he eats breakfast..." | order 3 pictures | loses order words (then/after), past tense |
- Each rung adds one cognitive element. Items for Cambridge-aligned wording can be built from the Starters grammar list above (can, there is/are, present continuous, prepositions, have got).
- For yes/no items use balanced keys (50 percent no) because guessing gives 50 percent.

### Gaps
- No published error-frequency data for young EFL listening by task type was found; the error patterns above are teaching-experience inferences.

## 4. Distinguishing the six learner profiles; minimum items per band for about 80 percent classification

### Takeaway
Use a staged/adaptive test: ~4-6 items per rung of the ladder, with a rule of "stop after two consecutive failed rungs". By binomial reasoning, 5-6 items per band with a cut-off of 4 or 5 correct gives about 80-90 percent classification between a child who "has" the skill (85 percent expected correct) and a child who does not (33-50 percent correct, incl. guessing).

### Cited Findings
- Cambridge Starters Listening itself uses 5 items per part, 20 items in ~20 minutes — [YLE handbook](https://assets.cambridgeenglish.org/network-services/yle-handbook-for-teachers.pdf)
- No published guidance on minimum items for young-learner placement bands was found in my search.

### Inferences (my own calculation, simple binomial model; assumptions: items independent, a child at/above band has p1 chance of correct, a child below has p0; balanced error costs; best cut-off chosen)
| Items per band | p0=0.33 (3-choice guess) vs p1=0.85 | p0=0.50 vs 0.85 | p0=0.60 vs 0.90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.89 (cut >=3) | 0.79 (>=3) | 0.76 (>=4) |
| 5 | 0.90 (>=4) | 0.82 (>=4) | 0.79 (>=4) |
| 6 | 0.93 (>=4) | 0.83 (>=5) | 0.83 (>=5) |
| 8 | 0.95 (>=5) | 0.88 (>=6) | 0.85 (>=7) |
| 10 | 0.97 (>=7) | 0.89 (>=7) | 0.88 (>=8) |
| 12 | 0.98 (>=8) | 0.92 (>=9) | 0.90 (>=10) |
- Rule of thumb: 3-choice picture items (guess 33 percent): 5 items per band is enough for ~90 percent; 2-choice/yes-no items (guess 50 percent): need 8+; adjacent-band distinctions with a true p0 of 0.6 (child nearly at the band) need 8-10. Hence "about 80 percent" is reachable with ~5-6 three-choice items per band, and the ~80 percent figure is for a single band decision; error compounds across a 6-band decision, so the realistic whole-profile accuracy with 5-6 items/band and adaptive routing is about 70-80 percent (my estimate, not computed).
- Profile -> evidence pattern (proposal):
  1. Never heard English: fails L1 noun items (<=2/5 on 3-choice, i.e. at chance) incl. cognates; stop test early (also keeps young child from frustration).
  2. Knows isolated words: passes L1 (>=4/5), fails L2-L4 (instructions, colour+noun).
  3. Understands simple instructions/questions: passes L2-L8, fails sequencing/wh- and letters.
  4. Reads letters/words: passes letter-name and letter-sound taps, word->picture CVC (>=4/5).
  5. Reads simple sentences: passes sentence->picture and 'the/is/I/a' sight word items.
  6. A2 readers: reads 20-60-word text with 3-4 comprehension questions; passes past-simple and wh- items.
- Caveat: young children (4-6) show high attention/engagement noise; add 1-2 warm-up items that never count and a short break rule; speed and consistency flags on repeat taps. Add a parent-reported age/schooling pre-screen to route start point.

### Gaps
- No published empirical validation for these cut scores in this context; recommend pilot with ~30-50 children per band to calibrate. Parameters p1 and p0 are assumptions.

## 5. Typical vocabulary-size growth for EFL children aged 4-9 versus hours

### Takeaway
Best-sourced figure: about 500 words per year in about 100 contact hours, i.e. ~5 words per hour, among Greek EFL learners (Milton). Other sources suggest primary EFL receptive vocabulary stays within the first 1,000 frequency band for years. Hence expect ~100-300 receptive words after 40-60 hours for beginners, ~500 after ~100 hours, ~1,000+ after ~200 hours.

### Cited Findings
- "About 500 words are learned every year in about 100 contact hours" (Milton 2006, Greek learners), reported with learners reaching Cambridge FCE with ~3,500 words — [Milton & Alexiou, GALA paper](https://www.enl.auth.gr/gala/14th/Papers/English%20papers/Milton&Alexiou.pdf) and search summary of the same (secondary).
- Young EFL learners' receptive vocabulary sizes lie "within the most frequent 1,000 words"; growth is "incremental and constant" — [Vocabulary_growth_2017](https://dosen.ikipsiliwangi.ac.id/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2021/02/Vocabulary_growth_2017.pdf) (search snippet).
- A study of 274 Spanish primary EFL learners after a total of 629 hours of instruction found receptive vocabulary "satisfactory" (exact numbers not retrieved) — [Univ. La Rioja record](https://investigacion.unirioja.es/documentos/5bbc5d4eb7506047d09b6711?lang=en).
- CEFR-linked vocabulary-size estimates (secondary summary, Milton): A1 under 1,500; A2 1,500-2,500; B1 2,750-3,250; B2 3,250-3,750 words (lexemes in the 5,000 frequency range, adults) — summary via search; original source not verified.
- Vocabulary instruction recommendation of ~10 words per hour (secondary, search snippet, unverified).

### Inferences
- Starters list (~500 words) corresponds to ~100 contact hours at Milton's rate; Movers cumulative (~900) ~180 hours; Flyers cumulative (~1,400) ~280 hours at 5 words/hour. Cambridge itself says the exams are 'for children aged 6-12', so a 4-6 year-old who knows 100-300 words after one year is on-track. Online 1:1 learners may exceed the rate; classroom learners with fewer hours fall below (my inference).
- Placement implication: a typical Pre-A1 child should be expected to know 50-300 receptive words (mostly concrete nouns from animals, colours, family, food, body); an A1 child 300-700 words and mostly Starters + part of Movers; an A2 child 700-1,400+.

### Gaps
- No source found on words known after N hours for ages 4-6 specifically; Milton figure is for older primary/secondary Greek learners. Per-hour rates vary widely with input quality and L1.
- The Milton and Vassiliou 2000 paper itself was not retrieved.
