## Q1. Lowest CEFR level in the English Grammar Profile for the listed structures

### Takeaway
Only a few EGP facts were verified from a primary source (the EGP authors' own paper): all four traditional conditionals are in stable learner use by B1, EGP recorded 27 different conditional forms/uses from A1 to C2, and EGP often finds competence earlier than textbook syllabi place it. Item-by-item EGP levels (e.g. "third conditional = B1/B2?") could not be retrieved; secondary level lists disagree with each other.

### Cited Findings
- EGP is a corpus-based inventory of over 1,200 grammar competency ("can-do") statements across A1-C2, built from the 55-million-word Cambridge Learner Corpus (exam scripts from 200+ countries, 140+ L1s); main authors O'Keeffe and Mark. — [O'Keeffe & Mark, EGP conference paper (Birmingham Corpus Linguistics 2017)](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/documents/college-artslaw/corpus/conference-archives/2017/general/paper300.pdf); also [IJCL 22(4)](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/journals/15699811/22/4)
- Past simple: at A1 EGP records only the affirmative form with "a limited range of regular and irregular verbs", for talking about everyday events or states (example: "Every time I went to Hendon Park, I saw so many people there", A1). Later levels add more verbs and more complex functions. So past simple affirmative is A1 in EGP, though restricted. — [O'Keeffe & Mark paper](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/documents/college-artslaw/corpus/conference-archives/2017/general/paper300.pdf)
- Conditionals: traditional syllabi introduce zero/first/second/third "one by one from A2 level to B2", but EGP learner data show "evidence of all four traditional clauses in stable use by B1" and 27 different forms/uses expressing conditionality from A1 to C2. — [O'Keeffe & Mark paper](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/documents/college-artslaw/corpus/conference-archives/2017/general/paper300.pdf)
- The same authors state EGP found "mismatches" between traditional syllabus levels and when structures are competently acquired, with learners sometimes showing competence before a structure appears on syllabi. — [O'Keeffe & Mark paper](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/documents/college-artslaw/corpus/conference-archives/2017/general/paper300.pdf)
- EGP is based on exam writing by learners (potential circularity acknowledged: learners prepare with exam materials). It measures when a form is used competently, not when it is taught or error-free. — [same paper](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/documents/college-artslaw/corpus/conference-archives/2017/general/paper300.pdf)
- SECONDARY (British Council / EQUALS Core Inventory as reproduced by ExamEnglish; NOT EGP): zero + 1st conditional A2; 2nd and 3rd conditional B1; mixed conditionals B2/C1; simple passive B1; "passives" B2; all passive forms C1; reported speech (range of tenses) B1; relative clauses B2; inversion with negative adverbials C1; "would" for past habits B2; wish B2, wish/if only regrets C1. — [ExamEnglish CEFR grammar](https://www.examenglish.com/CEFR/cefr_grammar.htm) (page states source is British Council/EQUALS, not EGP)
- SECONDARY (aggregator summaries, inconsistent with each other and with EQUALS): one lists A2 as including past simple, going to, comparatives, countable/uncountable, must, simple passive, second conditional, reported speech, while B1 gets present perfect, past continuous, first conditional — [internationalenglishtest.com / search summary of CEFR grammar sites](https://internationalenglishtest.com/blog/english-grammar-review-cefr-levels/); another puts zero conditional at A1, first at B1, second B2, third C1 — [summary from tracktest.eu](https://tracktest.eu/english-grammar-cef-level-requirements/). These are unreliable; they contradict each other (e.g. second conditional A2 vs B1 vs B2) and the EGP authors' finding that all four are stable by B1. Treat as evidence that level lists disagree, not as EGP data.

### Inferences
- Because EGP records competence rather than syllabus placement, a structure can have an EGP "first appears" level lower than the textbook level (e.g. conditionals, per the authors). A test item should be judged at the level of the specific EGP can-do (function + form + complexity), not the bare structure name: "past simple" is A1 for simple affirmative, while complex uses (e.g. in narrative with other tenses, negative/question range) sit higher.
- For conditionals, a safe teacher heuristic from the verified finding: simple, high-frequency "if + present, will" and "if + past, would" sentences can be fine at A2-B1; hypothetical complexity (third conditional with "would have", mixed, inverted "Had I known") is where B2-C1 enters. This is an inference, not a verified EGP entry.

### Gaps

## Q3. CEFR descriptors for reading/listening and Cambridge exam text lengths

### Takeaway
Cambridge exam format pages confirm test structure, question counts and that every recording is heard twice, but they did not state per-part word counts or speech rates; I found no verified words-per-minute figures. B2 First total reading load is 2,200-2,500 words.

### Cited Findings
- B2 First Reading and Use of English: 1 h 15 min, 52 questions, 7 parts, "2,200-2,500 words to read in total"; texts from newspapers, magazines, reports, fiction, advertisements, letters, messages, brochures. Listening: about 40 min, 30 questions, 4 parts (8 short MC; 10 sentence completion from monologue; 5 multiple matching; 7 interview MC); each recording heard twice. — [Cambridge English B2 First format](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/first/format/)
- B1 Preliminary Reading: 45 min, 32 questions, 6 parts (notices/real-world texts, longer articles for detailed comprehension, shorter texts for vocabulary/open cloze). Listening: 30 min + 6 min transfer, 25 questions, 4 parts (visual identification, gist from short conversations, gap-fill from a monologue, interview with attitude/opinion); recordings heard twice. — [Cambridge English B1 Preliminary format](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/preliminary/format/)
- A2 Key: Reading and Writing 1 h, 32 questions over 7 parts (MC, vocabulary cloze from a factual text, open cloze email, guided writing, picture story); Listening 30 min including 6 min transfer, 25 questions, 5 parts; each recording heard twice. The page does not give word counts for passages or recording durations. — [Cambridge English A2 Key format](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/key/format/)
- Writing targets: B1 Preliminary about 100-120 words; B2 First about 140-190 words (from search-result summary of Cambridge format pages). — [Cambridge English exam formats](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/preliminary/format/)

### Inferences
- B2 First's 2,200-2,500 words across 52 reading questions / 7 parts is the only hard quantity verified; per-text lengths and listening WPM remain unsourced.

## Q4. Commonly mis-levelled structures

### Takeaway
The one verified, authoritative point: EGP authors say conditionals (all four) are in stable learner use by B1 although syllabi stagger them A2-B2, and that EGP competence evidence can predate syllabus placement. Beyond that I could not verify specific "people think B1 but EGP says A2" cases.

### Cited Findings
- All four traditional conditionals show stable use by B1 in EGP data, versus syllabi introducing them from A2 to B2. — [O'Keeffe & Mark paper](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/documents/college-artslaw/corpus/conference-archives/2017/general/paper300.pdf)
- Published CEFR grammar lists disagree on where structures sit (e.g. second conditional shown at A2, B1 or B2; third conditional B1 or C1 depending on the page), showing level-by-structure lists from non-EGP sources are not interchangeable. — compare [ExamEnglish (EQUALS-based)](https://www.examenglish.com/CEFR/cefr_grammar.htm) with [tracktest.eu summary](https://tracktest.eu/english-grammar-cef-level-requirements/)

### Inferences
- Practical rule for reviewers: label an item by the specific EGP can-do (function plus form plus lexical/complexity load) rather than the structure's name, and treat high vocabulary load or abstract content as pushing an item above the grammar's level.

### Gaps
- No verified list of specific mis-levelled structures; no access to Hawkins & Filipovic (2012) "Criterial Features in L2 English" content; EGP Online unavailable. Suggested follow-up: query EGP Online manually for each structure listed in Q1, and check OALD/EVP badges for Q2 words.
