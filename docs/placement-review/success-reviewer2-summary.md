## Overall assessment

Strengths: sensible A1 to C1 spread, mostly clean keys, good use of real learner errors in many grammar items (since/for, don't have to/mustn't, "informations/advices", to seeing, rise/raise), good B2/C1 reading and listening (su-54, su-58, su-59, su-73, su-75).

Main weaknesses:
1. Skill balance. About 76% of items are Use-of-English (grammar ~35, vocabulary ~21 and the pragmatics items). Reading is 10 items and listening 9, spread thin per level (A2 listening 2, B1 3, B2 3, C1 1). For a 20-36 item adaptive test that is too little receptive evidence at any single level.
2. Redundancy in grammar: counterfactuals take 8 items (su-17, 39, 46, 65, 66, 21, 79 plus inversion 64, 65, 67), including three negative/conditional inversions at C1 (64, 65, 67), so C1 grammar is almost only inversion. Verb patterns (43, 47), relatives (24, 41), and register/pragmatics (15, 36, 37, 38, 60, 62, 63) repeat.
3. Missing structures: articles, prepositions of time/place, question formation, comparatives beyond one item, quantifiers, modals of deduction, causative, future perfect/continuous, used to / be used to, passive reporting, participle clauses, discourse markers, word formation, and non-office contexts (travel, health, shopping, social).
4. Test-wiseness: in nearly every reading, listening and register item the key is the longest, most polished option; the distractors are cartoon-rude or garbled (su-36, 37, 38, 60, 62, 63; su-31, 33, 55, 73). Several pragmatics items are solved by "pick the polite-sounding sentence" with no language knowledge.
5. Level inflation. Grammar tagged B2 at 0.72-0.82 is often B1+ (su-39, 41, 43, 44, 48, 66, 69). Difficulty is not spaced with enough low-B2 to separate B1 from B2.
6. Business content: pitched about right in most lexis (supplier, quote, targets), but jargon creeps in ("tender", "scope") and about 90% of items are office-based, so the "general English core" is thin. Two vocabulary items test grammar (su-25), and the "fluency" label is wrong for functional MC items.
7. American English: "car park" and "1 March" (su-32), "tender" (su-55), "shall I" (su-33), "main hall" (su-34), "weigh up" (su-51), British "fill in" vs "fill out" (su-08).
8. Cognate/L1 effects: feedback, calendar, valid, lucid, implications, contradict, postponed are cognates in ES/FR/IT, so Romance speakers will score higher than their level on these.
