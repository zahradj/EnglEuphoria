## 1. Structure, task types, items and timing of Pre A1 Starters / A1 Movers / A2 Flyers (what needs reading, what does not)

### Takeaway
Starters (Pre A1) is about 45 min total: Listening about 20 min / 20 items, Reading&Writing 20 min / 25 items, Speaking 3-5 min. Listening and Speaking at Starters need NO reading (draw lines, write a spelled name/number, tick one of 3 pictures, colour, point, place cards). All of Reading&Writing needs letter/word recognition (copy, spell, one-word answers) but is picture-supported. Nobody fails: 1-5 shields per skill.

### Cited Findings
- Cambridge says the tests are "designed for young learners aged 6-12"; the Collins guide says 7-12 and Starters "aimed at students aged 7+" (minor source conflict) — [CAM-INFO](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/in/Images/153312-yle-information-for-candidates.pdf); [COL-TG](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Samples/ELT/74863_Pre_A1_Starters_Teacher's_Guide.pdf). Neither targets 4-5 year olds.
- Total lengths: Starters about 45 min; Movers about 1 h; Flyers about 1 h 14 min — [CAM-INFO](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/in/Images/153312-yle-information-for-candidates.pdf)
- No pass/fail; certificate shows 1-5 shields per skill (listening, reading&writing, speaking) — [CAM-INFO](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/in/Images/153312-yle-information-for-candidates.pdf)
- Recordings are played twice; each part begins with one or two examples — [CAM-START](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/starters/test-format/); [CAM-MOVERS](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/young-learners/paper/movers/format/)

**Starters LISTENING (about 20 min, 4 parts, 20 items, 5 per part)** — [COL-TG](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Samples/ELT/74863_Pre_A1_Starters_Teacher's_Guide.pdf); [CAM-INFO](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/in/Images/153312-yle-information-for-candidates.pdf)
- P1: big scene picture, 7 names printed around it; hears adult-child dialogue; DRAWS LINES from names to the people (skills: names, clothes, appearance, present continuous). 1 example.
- P2: picture + dialogue; WRITES a name or number. Names are spelled aloud in the recording; numbers may be digit or word. 2 examples. Needs numbers 1-20 and letter names.
- P3: five short dialogues, each with 3-option picture multiple choice (A/B/C); TICK a box. 1 example. No reading.
- P4: big picture with 7 identical objects (e.g. robots); hears position preposition + colour and COLOURS 5 objects. Neatness not marked, only right object + right colour. No reading.
- So P1, P3, P4 need no letter reading; P2 needs writing from dictation.

**Starters READING & WRITING (20 min, 5 parts, 25 items, 5 per part)** — [COL-TG](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Samples/ELT/74863_Pre_A1_Starters_Teacher's_Guide.pdf)
- P1: 5 pictures + sentence "This is a… / These are…"; tick (true) or cross (false). 2 examples.
- P2: one big picture + 5 sentences; write "yes" or "no".
- P3: 5 pictures each with an anagram and dashes showing letter count; write the word (e.g. kite, train, robot, lorry, helicopter).
- P4: short gapped text about a picture; 8 small labelled pictures (words) below, choose 5 and COPY into gaps.
- P5: 3-picture story, 5 Wh-/How many questions; one-word answers (noun, verb or number).

**Starters SPEAKING (3-5 min, one-to-one; an "usher" brings child in; Cambridge parent summary lists 4 parts, Collins guide table lists 5)** — [CAM-INFO](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/in/Images/153312-yle-information-for-candidates.pdf); [COL-TG](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Samples/ELT/74863_Pre_A1_Starters_Teacher's_Guide.pdf); [CAM-START](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/starters/test-format/)
- P1: scene picture; child POINTS to named things ("Where's the mirror?"), then places 3 object cards following spoken instructions with prepositions ("Put the lemon next to the lamp") — comprehension only, no speech needed.
- P2: scene picture; short answers to "What's this? What colour is it? How many dolls? What's the bird doing?" + one "Tell me about the girl."
- P3: 3 object cards; short answers ("What's this? Have you got a rubber?").
- P4: personal questions ("Can you play tennis? What's your favourite food? How do you go to school?"). Back-up questions (yes/no, "Is it pink or blue?") scaffold hesitant children.

**A1 MOVERS (about 1 h)** — [CAM-INFO](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/in/Images/153312-yle-information-for-candidates.pdf); [CAM-MOVERS](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/young-learners/paper/movers/format/)
- Listening about 25 min, 5 parts, 25 items: P1 draw lines; P2 write words and numbers (place, trip, party); P3 match people/objects in one set to pictures in another by writing letters in boxes; P4 tick 3-option pictures; P5 colour parts of a picture + write something.
- Reading&Writing 30 min, 6 parts, 35 items: P1 match pictures/words to describing sentences, copy words; P2 circle A/B/C conversational replies; P3 gapped story (copy words) + choose title (tick); P4 gapped text copy words; P5 find words in a story to complete sentences (1-3 words); P6 complete sentences, answer questions, write sentences about a picture. Spelling must be correct.
- Speaking 5-7 min, 4 parts: find differences between two pictures; tell a picture story; "odd one out" among 4 pictures; personal questions.

**A2 FLYERS (about 1 h 14)** — [CAM-INFO](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/in/Images/153312-yle-information-for-candidates.pdf)
- Listening about 25 min, same 5 part types as Movers. Reading&Writing 40 min, 7 parts (match words to definitions; complete conversation by writing a letter; gapped story + title; gapped text; story sentences with 1-4 words; diary/message gap-fill; write a short story from 3 pictures). Speaking 7-9 min, 4 parts incl. asking/answering questions about two similar situations.

### Inferences
- Reading is needed only from Starters R&W; a pre-reader (about 4-6) can sit the Listening-style tasks and Speaking P1 only. For a no-reading placement, mirror Starters Listening P1/P3/P4 and Speaking P1 (point / place card).
- 5 items per part with a change of physical action each part is Cambridge's way of managing attention.

### Gaps
- Official Young Learners Handbook (young-learners-handbook-2018.pdf) was unreadable/404 for me; per-part timings within a paper not published in sources read.

## 2. Cambridge Placement Test for Young Learners and Oxford Placement Test for Young Learners

### Takeaway
Cambridge's YL placement test is a short computer-adaptive test reusing Starters/Movers/Flyers task types for LISTENING and READING&WRITING only (no speaking) and reports which of Pre A1 Starters / A1 Movers / A2 Flyers the child is ready to prepare for. It needs mouse and keyboard (typing words), so it presupposes basic literacy. OUP's test is also adaptive, ages 7-12, about 40-45 items, CEFR Pre-A1 to B1 + score out of 80.

### Cited Findings (Cambridge)
- Purpose: place students into classes and Starters/Movers/Flyers courses; mixed-ability grouping; choose materials — [CAM-PT](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/yle-placement-test)
- Format: Listening = four parts, five questions each, example in each part, each part heard twice. Reading&Writing = four parts, up to 10 questions each, example in each part. Covers listening, reading, writing — [CAM-PT](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/yle-placement-test)
- Computer adaptive: level of questions changes with answers; students may not see every task type; automatic marking, immediate results, report shows the qualification the student is ready to prepare for — [CAM-PT](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/yle-placement-test)
- Listening formats: (a) hear dialogue, pick 1 of 3 pictures (click circle under picture); (b) past-tense dialogue, pick picture and choose the day from pop-up menu; (c) listening for numbers/spelling, type into spaces by a context picture; (d) type names/spellings next to written prompts — [CAM-PT](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/yle-placement-test)
- Reading&Writing formats: type unscrambled word (one letter per box) for a pictured object; read dialogue and choose correct reply from 3; read gapped text and type words from pictured prompts; read story and complete sentences with 1-3 words; true/false (yes/no) about a picture; gapped short text with 3-option dropdown — [CAM-PT](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/yle-placement-test)
- Not published in the guide: minutes, stopping rule, reliability/validity statistics, parent-administration advice (searched; found none).

### Cited Findings (Oxford, via search summaries; OUP page itself not fetchable)
- Oxford Placement Test for Young Learners: ages 7-12; two sections, Language Use (vocabulary, functions, grammar; 18 questions) and Listening (gist/detail, short and extended; 12 questions); computer-adaptive; "40-45 questions depending on level"; teacher sets timer 50-90 min; result = CEFR level (Pre-A1 to B1), score out of 80, time taken — https://eltpreprod.oup.com/feature/global/young-learners-placement (via search snippet)
- ELT Weekly overview says 30-40 min and describes "listening and reading comprehension" — [ELT Weekly](https://eltweekly.com/2024/10/oxford-placement-test-for-young-learners-an-overview/). This conflicts with 18+12=30 vs 40-45 items; UNVERIFIED which is right.

### Pearson
- Pearson English Benchmark Young Learners: ages 6-13, Pre-A1 to B1, online (tablet/laptop), 45 min or less, game-like activities; five levels x three tests; four skills except Level 1 which has no writing; AI-scored — https://www.pearson.com/english/assessment/english-benchmark-young-learners.html (search summary only; unverified detail).

### Inferences
- Shared design across Cambridge/Oxford/Pearson: adaptive, picture-supported, 30-45 min, listening-led, CEFR band + next-course level. None validated or aimed at 4-5 year olds.

### Gaps
- No reliability coefficients, SEM or validity studies found for any placement test (OUP "recognized for validity" is marketing language). Cambridge research chapters (Papp, Bailey, Hasselgreen) not reached; only an abstract hit: https://researchportal.beds.ac.uk/p/en/research-outputs/the-cognitive-validity-of-tests-of-listening-and-speaking-designe-3 (matches young-learner processing limits to Cambridge YL task features).

## 3. Vocabulary and grammar scope at Pre A1 / A1 / A2

### Cited Findings
- Starters official vocabulary list (2018) is thematic: Animals (about 30 words: bear, cat, cow, crocodile, dog, elephant, giraffe, hippo, monkey, snake, zebra, zoo...), Body & face (arm, ear, eye, face, foot, hair, hand, head, leg, mouth, nose, smile), Clothes (hat, jacket, jeans, shirt, shoe, shorts, skirt, sock, T-shirt, wear...), Colours (11: black, blue, brown, green, grey, orange, pink, purple, red, white, yellow), Family & friends, Food & drink (apple, banana, bread, cake, carrot, egg, ice cream, juice, milk...), The home, Numbers 1-20, Places & directions (behind, between, in front of, next to, on, under), School (book, draw, letter, listen, look, name, open, read, write...), plus further topics (names of children such as Alex, Ann, Ben, Bill, Jill, Mr White) — [COL-TG](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Samples/ELT/74863_Pre_A1_Starters_Teacher's_Guide.pdf) (only the first pages of the list read; exact total word count not found).
- Word lists for each level downloadable from Cambridge; Flyers: https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/pl/Images/149681-yle-flyers-word-list.pdf (not opened) — [CAM-INFO](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/in/Images/153312-yle-information-for-candidates.pdf)
- Skills/grammar (Cambridge parent summary): all levels — know and spell word-list words; present simple and present continuous; descriptions of people (doing, wearing, looks); prepositions of place; write spelled/heard words; copy words; follow colouring instructions; ask/answer simple questions. Starters adds — unjumble letters. Movers and Flyers add — past simple; picture-story telling; differences between pictures. Flyers adds — past continuous, "going to", "will" — [CAM-INFO](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/in/Images/153312-yle-information-for-candidates.pdf)
- Starters structures seen: This is a… / These are…; Is it…? Are these…?; What colour…? How many…? Where's…? What's X doing?; Have you got…? Can you…? — [COL-TG](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Samples/ELT/74863_Pre_A1_Starters_Teacher's_Guide.pdf)
- Starters test-instruction words children must know: choose, colour, draw, listen, look, read, see, tick, write; box, cross, example, letter, line, name, number, picture, question, story, word — [COL-TG](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Samples/ELT/74863_Pre_A1_Starters_Teacher's_Guide.pdf)

### Gaps
- Official handbook grammar syllabus and word-count totals not read.

## 4. Item-design guidance (options, pictures, audio, practice, fatigue)

### Cited Findings (observable design choices; no explicit research rule found)
- 3-option picture multiple choice is the standard in Starters/Movers listening and in the placement test — [COL-TG](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Samples/ELT/74863_Pre_A1_Starters_Teacher's_Guide.pdf); [CAM-PT](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/yle-placement-test)
- Every part starts with a worked example (some have two); listening heard twice; fixed rubrics so children learn the instructions — [COL-TG](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Samples/ELT/74863_Pre_A1_Starters_Teacher's_Guide.pdf); [CAM-PT](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/yle-placement-test)
- Only 5 items per part, with action changes (draw/write/tick/colour/point/place) — [COL-TG](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Samples/ELT/74863_Pre_A1_Starters_Teacher's_Guide.pdf)
- Fair marking: colour neatness not marked; names spelled aloud so spelling isn't tested; numbers as digits or words — [COL-TG](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Samples/ELT/74863_Pre_A1_Starters_Teacher's_Guide.pdf); [CAM-INFO](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/in/Images/153312-yle-information-for-candidates.pdf)
- Affective design: "nobody fails", shields, friendly examiner, back-up questions, child may ask "Sorry, can you say that again?"; children answer all questions even if unsure; candidates may not ask questions during listening — [CAM-INFO](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/in/Images/153312-yle-information-for-candidates.pdf)
- Speaking P1 tests comprehension via pointing/placing, with no spoken output — [COL-TG](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Samples/ELT/74863_Pre_A1_Starters_Teacher's_Guide.pdf)

### Gaps
- No published rule found on max number of options for 4-6 year olds, picture size, or fatigue limits; the 3-option convention and ~45 min total for ages 6+ are the best evidence. "Assessing Young Learners" volume not accessed.

## 5. Can-do statements: Pre A1 vs A1 vs A2 for young learners

### Cited Findings — Cambridge placement guide table "The learner CAN…" — [CAM-PT](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/yle-placement-test)
(The PDF table extracted jumbled; level allocation below is partly my reconstruction — flag.)
- Listening: Starters = understand simple sentences, follow simple instructions, understand very simple descriptions of everyday objects. Movers = understand instructions of more than one sentence, simple conversations on everyday topics, very short stories. Flyers = understand some very short conversations and simple stories with picture help.
- Reading/writing: Starters = recognise and write alphabet letters, copy words/phrases/short sentences, understand simple written sentences, spell name and simple words. Movers = understand simple written descriptions, write short sentences about likes/dislikes, give personal details. Flyers = understand simple signs/notices and short texts even without knowing every word, link sentences with and/because/then, write short stories from pictures.
- Speaking: Starters = respond to simple questions, name familiar people/things. Movers = describe objects/pictures/actions, answer questions about school, tell a very simple story with pictures. Flyers = ask basic questions, talk about things they have done, tell a story from pictures or own ideas.
- Cambridge Scale chart: Pre A1 Starters lowest, A1 Movers next, A2 Flyers below A2 Key for Schools — [CAM-PT](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/yle-placement-test)

### Inferences
- Pre A1 child: understands single sentences/instructions, names objects, copies letters/words; A1: two-sentence instructions, short personal sentences, past simple; A2: connects ideas, past continuous/future, asks questions.

### Gaps
- Council of Europe Companion Volume has no young-learner scales (as stated in the task); I did not find a Cambridge "Young Learners and the CEFR" document, so Cambridge's own can-do table is the only descriptor source here.
