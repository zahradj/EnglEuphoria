# Academy curriculum — B1

> Generated. 16 Seasons × 8 = **128 live lessons** of 60 minutes (cumulative live hours from Pre-A1: **328**). Plan rule: every lesson has an approved one-to-one plan first (`docs/academy-lesson-plan-template.md`) with research logged in `docs/research/`.

## 1. Level at a glance
- **CEFR:** B1 (paraphrase): copes with most situations while travelling; produces simple connected text on familiar topics; describes experiences, events, hopes and ambitions; gives reasons and opinions.
- **The student:** An intermediate teen who wants to express opinions, tell experiences and handle school-like tasks in English.
- **Method:** Task-first (Mission before the rule); mediation starts: every Season has one task; present perfect split by sub-use.
- **Supports:** First words of a frame; hints on request.
- **Input texts:** ~150-300 words [target].
- **Daily 10:** 10 minutes plus an extensive-reading target of a short text.

## 4. Every Season has the same eight lessons
Cold Open (hook, gist) · Word Lab (vocabulary and pronunciation) · Pattern Lab (grammar noticing) · On Air (real exchange) · Deep Dive (longer text and short writing) · Side Quest (student's pick, light new language) · Remix (zero new language) · Finale (peer task, Release, checkpoint, zero new language). Run of show: standard 60 minutes (Pre-A1 uses its own slower one).

## 5. Pronunciation and L1 priorities
- Weak forms and connected speech
- Present perfect vs past simple (Spanish, French, Italian transfer)
- Prepositions
- Spelling of common words

Season pronunciation focus:
- B1-S01: contracted 'have' /əv/ and 'been' /bɪn/ vs /biːn/; intonation of surprise
- B1-S02: "used to" /juːst tə/ in connected speech; sentence stress in contrasts
- B1-S03: for /fə/ and since /sɪns/ in connected speech; question intonation in interviews
- B1-S04: news-reader rhythm and sentence stress; past participle forms (said, been, seen)
- B1-S05: intonation and pause after the if-clause; contracted will /l/
- B1-S06: 'd /d/ (I'd, she'd) vs 'would'; stress for contrast
- B1-S07: past participle endings /t/ /d/ /ɪd/; stress in compound nouns
- B1-S08: pausing and chunking before relative pronouns; stress on the new information
- B1-S09: modal verbs: strong vs weak forms; intonation of doubt
- B1-S10: stress on time expressions; contracted will in promises (I'll be there)
- B1-S11: -ing and to + verb: weak forms; stress and intonation in opinions
- B1-S12: reporting verbs: said /sɛd/, told /təʊld/; sentence rhythm in reported clauses
- B1-S13: 'd /d/ for had in connected speech; dramatic pausing and stress
- B1-S14: wish /wɪʃ/ - final /ʃ/; warm, supportive intonation
- B1-S15: don't have to vs mustn't: stress and vowel; linking words: although /ɔːlˈðəʊ/
- B1-S16: rising and falling tags (confirmation vs real questions); stress in phrasal verbs

## 11. Season details
### B1-S01 Have You Ever...?
*Vee dares the group to complete a bucket list in one term. Who has already done the most?*
- I can talk about experiences I have had or never had.
- I can ask others about their experiences and respond with interest.
- Lexical fields: adventure and activities; feelings about experiences; places and countries visited
- Mediation: Relay to a friend, in simple English, the main points of a short article about an unusual experience.
- Skins: travel, gaming, music, fitness

### B1-S02 Then and Now
*Theo finds his old phone with photos from five years ago. Compare then and now - and spot what has changed.*
- I can describe past habits and states and compare them with now.
- I can understand a short text about how life has changed.
- Lexical fields: childhood and school memories; technology then and now; town and lifestyle changes
- Mediation: Summarise for a younger cousin a short text about how a daily habit has changed.
- Skins: tech, music, film, food

### B1-S03 Still Going
*Ava has kept the same hobby for six years and Mia has never kept anything for six weeks. Who is telling the truth?*
- I can say how long something has been true.
- I can ask and answer questions about duration.
- Lexical fields: hobbies, clubs and habits; time periods and dates; relationships and routines
- Mediation: Pass on to a friend, in plain English, what a person said in a short interview.
- Skins: football, gaming, art, animals, music

### B1-S04 Breaking News
*A rumour about the school is spreading through Ava's group chat. Is it true? Choose the right tense and report the news without making it worse.*
- I can report recent news and say when something happened.
- I can choose between the present perfect and the past simple for a news story.
- Lexical fields: news and media; events and results; accidents and surprises
- Mediation: Turn a short written news item into a spoken summary for someone who has not read it.
- Skins: social, football, film, science

### B1-S05 If This, Then That
*Mia is designing a board game and the rules keep breaking. Fix them with the right if-sentences.*
- I can say what always happens and what will happen if something happens.
- I can explain rules and instructions clearly.
- Lexical fields: games and rules; cause and effect; instructions and warnings
- Mediation: Explain the rules of a game described in a short text to someone who has never played it.
- Skins: gaming, tech, football, science

### B1-S06 What If...?
*Vee asks one impossible question each morning. Today: what would you do if you could be invisible for a day?*
- I can talk about imaginary situations and what I would do.
- I can give reasons for my choices and respond to others.
- Lexical fields: dilemmas and choices; superpowers and fantasy; money, fame and school life
- Mediation: Summarise two different answers to a dilemma for a third person.
- Skins: film, gaming, anime, science

### B1-S07 Made in the World
*Theo wants to know where his sneakers, his phone and his lunch really come from. Trace one product from start to finish.*
- I can describe how something is made or done without saying who did it.
- I can understand a short factual text about a process.
- Lexical fields: manufacturing and materials; food production; inventions and discoveries
- Mediation: Relay the steps of a process from a diagram or text to a friend who is trying to do it.
- Skins: fashion, food, tech, science

### B1-S08 Who's Who
*Mia's guessing game goes wrong when two people turn out to have the same description. Add details until only one fits.*
- I can add detail to a description using who, which, that and where.
- I can ask for and give clarification.
- Lexical fields: people and personalities; places and objects; definitions and descriptions
- Mediation: Explain what an unfamiliar object or person is, using a short description from a text.
- Skins: film, music, animals, anime

### B1-S09 Case Closed
*A trophy has disappeared from the school hall and Vee asks you to lead the investigation with Ava and Theo. Gather the clues and work out what must have happened, or might have.*
- I can say how certain I am about something using must, might and can't.
- I can explain my reasoning using evidence.
- Lexical fields: crime and mystery; clues and evidence; suspects and alibis
- Mediation: Summarise a witness account for the detective in your own words.
- Skins: film, gaming, anime, science

### B1-S10 Plans and Promises
*Vee's class trip is on but nobody has booked anything. Make arrangements with Mia and Theo and promise what you will do.*
- I can talk about arrangements I have made.
- I can make promises and simple predictions about the future.
- Lexical fields: travel and booking; diaries and appointments; promises and commitments
- Mediation: Pass on the key details of a booking message to a friend who must act on it.
- Skins: travel, music, football, food

### B1-S11 Learning Machines
*Vee's class is testing an AI tutor. Is it better to enjoy learning with it or to avoid using it too much? Decide.*
- I can give my opinion about technology and say what I enjoy, avoid or hope to do.
- I can write a short review with reasons.
- Lexical fields: technology and AI; learning and study habits; opinion adjectives
- Mediation: Explain the pros and cons given in a short text to a classmate who must decide.
- Skins: tech, gaming, science, social

### B1-S12 Word on the Street
*A message went through Ava, Theo, Mia and three others and arrived completely different. Trace it back and find who changed the story.*
- I can report what someone told me or said to me.
- I can change pronouns and time words when I report.
- Lexical fields: communication verbs (say, tell, ask); messages and social media; gossip and rumours
- Mediation: Pass on a voicemail message accurately and politely to the person it is for.
- Skins: social, film, music, gaming

### B1-S13 Before the Storm
*Ava, Theo, Mia and Vee arrive at a cabin and find the table set for dinner. Someone had been there. Work out the story behind it.*
- I can tell a story that includes what had happened earlier.
- I can organise a story with linkers (after, before, when).
- Lexical fields: storytelling and suspense; houses, forests and weather; emotions in narratives
- Mediation: Summarise a short story for someone who missed it.
- Skins: film, anime, gaming, nature

### B1-S14 Wish List
*Ava keeps a wish list she is afraid to show anyone. Can you encourage her to share it?*
- I can say what I wish were different about my life or world.
- I can express feelings about things I cannot change.
- Lexical fields: school, home and friendships; feelings and personal growth; society and environment
- Mediation: Relay a friend's wish to a teacher in a polite and clear way.
- Skins: music, art, social, nature

### B1-S15 Study Abroad
*An exchange student arrives next week and Vee asks Mia and Theo to prepare the guide. Write the one that will save their first month.*
- I can explain what is necessary and what is not, and give advice to a visitor.
- I can write an informal email and use linkers to contrast ideas.
- Lexical fields: school life abroad; customs and etiquette; accommodation and daily life
- Mediation: Explain the school rules in a leaflet to a new student who does not read English well.
- Skins: travel, food, fashion, football

### B1-S16 Teen Talk Show
*Vee has given you a late-night slot. Interview your guests, check facts and keep the audience on your side.*
- I can keep a conversation going, check information and show interest.
- I can use common phrasal verbs naturally in everyday talk.
- Lexical fields: entertainment and media; everyday phrasal verbs; interview language
- Mediation: Introduce a guest by relaying key facts from a profile, and summarise their answers for the audience.
- Skins: film, music, social, gaming, football
