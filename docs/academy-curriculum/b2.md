# Academy curriculum — B2

> Generated. 16 Seasons × 8 = **128 live lessons** of 60 minutes (cumulative live hours from Pre-A1: **456**). Plan rule: every lesson has an approved one-to-one plan first (`docs/academy-lesson-plan-template.md`) with research logged in `docs/research/`.

## 1. Level at a glance
- **CEFR:** B2 (paraphrase): understands the main ideas of complex text; interacts with fluency and spontaneity; produces clear, detailed text and explains a viewpoint with advantages and disadvantages.
- **The student:** An upper-intermediate student who defends opinions, summarises and mediates, and prepares for study in English.
- **Method:** Student-led tasks, opinion defence, summarising and mediation every Season; supports off by default (optional).
- **Supports:** None by default; optional on request.
- **Input texts:** ~300-500 words [target].
- **Daily 10:** 10 minutes plus an extensive-reading and listening target.

## 4. Every Season has the same eight lessons
Cold Open (hook, gist) · Word Lab (vocabulary and pronunciation) · Pattern Lab (grammar noticing) · On Air (real exchange) · Deep Dive (longer text and short writing) · Side Quest (student's pick, light new language) · Remix (zero new language) · Finale (peer task, Release, checkpoint, zero new language). Run of show: standard 60 minutes (Pre-A1 uses its own slower one).

## 5. Pronunciation and L1 priorities
- Stress and rhythm in longer utterances
- Collocations and false friends
- Register control
- Article nuance

Season pronunciation focus:
- B2-S01: 've been /əv bɪn/ in connected speech; sentence stress in progress reports
- B2-S02: have /əv/ in connected speech (would've, could've); emphasis for regret
- B2-S03: weak forms of auxiliary verbs (been, being); stress in long noun phrases
- B2-S04: stress and intonation in indirect questions; rhythm in long reported clauses
- B2-S05: commas as pauses before who/which; falling tone on extra information
- B2-S06: perfect modals: weak "have" /əv/; intonation for politeness
- B2-S07: "will have" /wɪl əv/ in connected speech; rhythm in long future clauses
- B2-S08: contrastive stress in arguments; intonation for conceding and challenging
- B2-S09: politeness intonation in indirect questions; linking in "have it done"
- B2-S10: stress on key conditions; intonation in polite bargaining
- B2-S11: verb + to: weak "to" /tə/; stress for contrast in near-identical pairs
- B2-S12: nuclear stress and focus; pause and emphasis in speeches
- B2-S13: intonation in long compressed sentences; stress on key content words
- B2-S14: stress and rhythm in comparison pairs; intonation in lists and numbers
- B2-S15: clear chunking for relayed information; neutral and polite tone
- B2-S16: stress and pace in a speech; signposting intonation

## 11. Season details
### B2-S01 Been There, Doing That
*Everyone in Vee's class has been working on a personal project all term. Whose has actually moved forward?*
- I can describe an activity that has been going on and its present results.
- I can give a clear, detailed account of progress to an audience.
- Lexical fields: projects and goals; effort and progress; study and sport
- Mediation: Summarise for the group the progress described in a long message from a team member.
- Skins: fitness, tech, music, art, gaming

### B2-S02 What Might Have Been
*Theo failed his driving test by one point. Explore the five moments that might have changed it - and what he should have done.*
- I can talk about past situations and how they could have been different.
- I can express regret, relief and criticism appropriately.
- Lexical fields: decisions and consequences; exams, sports and relationships; emotions about the past
- Mediation: Relay a friend's regretful story to a teacher while keeping the key reasons.
- Skins: film, football, gaming, music

### B2-S03 Passive Power
*A school report says 'mistakes were made' - but nobody says by whom. Ava is investigating for the school paper; help her see how the passive hides and reveals people.*
- I can describe processes and events when the agent is unknown or unimportant.
- I can write a formal factual report.
- Lexical fields: systems and institutions; science and technology; news and public events
- Mediation: Summarise a technical process from a text for someone with no background.
- Skins: tech, science, business, nature

### B2-S04 Reporting It
*Mia's interview notes are mixed up. Work out who asked what and who answered - and report it so the truth survives.*
- I can report questions, requests and commands accurately.
- I can choose suitable reporting verbs.
- Lexical fields: interviews and journalism; reporting verbs; attitudes and opinions
- Mediation: Report a long conversation to someone who was not there, selecting the key points.
- Skins: social, film, music, business

### B2-S05 Who Is He Anyway?
*Mia must write a profile of a mysterious school donor for the yearbook. Add the extra details that tell the real story.*
- I can add non-essential information to a sentence in a natural way.
- I can write a lively, detailed profile.
- Lexical fields: people and achievements; careers and biography; places and institutions
- Mediation: Condense a long biography into a short introduction for a speaker.
- Skins: music, football, science, film, business

### B2-S06 Could Have, Should Have
*Ava forgot to back up her project and lost everything. What should she have done - and what could her team have done for her?*
- I can speculate about what happened and give polite criticism.
- I can use modals to express different degrees of possibility about the past.
- Lexical fields: mistakes and consequences; teamwork and projects; tech problems
- Mediation: Give feedback on a friend's plan to a teacher in a balanced, tactful way.
- Skins: tech, gaming, football, business

### B2-S07 The Future Is...
*Vee's time-capsule letter from 2025 asks: what will you be doing in 2035? Answer it - and prove it is realistic.*
- I can say what will be happening or will be completed at a future time.
- I can write a clear, detailed text about my future plans.
- Lexical fields: future technology and society; careers and ambitions; time expressions for the future
- Mediation: Summarise two different predictions for the same topic from two sources.
- Skins: science, tech, nature, business

### B2-S08 Opinions That Matter
*Vee runs a weekly debate club. This week's motion: 'School should start later.' Convince the room - politely.*
- I can state and support an opinion with reasons and examples.
- I can soften or strengthen what I say to suit the audience.
- Lexical fields: education and society; media and technology debates; opinion and argument language
- Mediation: Summarise both sides of a debate fairly for a judge who missed it.
- Skins: social, tech, fitness, music, business

### B2-S09 Get It Done
*Ava's phone screen is cracked, her laptop is slow and her bike is stolen. She needs help - and the shops are not making it easy.*
- I can say that I arranged for someone else to do something.
- I can ask and answer polite indirect questions.
- Lexical fields: repairs, services and shopping; complaints and refunds; customer service language
- Mediation: Help a younger sibling understand a warranty or return policy.
- Skins: tech, fashion, business, travel

### B2-S10 Unless You Do
*Ava and Theo want the same band room. Negotiate a deal that works for both - with conditions neither can break.*
- I can state conditions and negotiate an agreement.
- I can use a range of conditional structures to be precise.
- Lexical fields: negotiation and agreements; rules and fairness; money and time
- Mediation: Mediate between two people who have different needs by suggesting a compromise.
- Skins: business, music, gaming, football

### B2-S11 Remember That!
*Theo remembers locking the door. Mia remembers to lock the door. Who is right, and why does the difference matter?*
- I can use common verbs followed by -ing or to and understand the change in meaning.
- I can make accurate choices about articles and generic reference in a formal text.
- Lexical fields: memory and psychology; habits and self-improvement; language and meaning
- Mediation: Explain a short scientific text on memory to a friend in simpler words.
- Skins: science, fitness, music, tech

### B2-S12 The Big Picture
*Theo says it was not the goal that won the match - it was the pass before it. Work out what really matters in the story.*
- I can give emphasis to the part of a sentence that matters most.
- I can structure a talk to guide the listener.
- Lexical fields: sport and teamwork; argument and persuasion; media and speeches
- Mediation: Pick out the main point of a long speech and re-express it in two sentences.
- Skins: football, film, social, music

### B2-S13 Cut the Words
*Vee has set a challenge: write a news story in half the words without losing anything important. How short can you go?*
- I can write more concisely using participle clauses and ellipsis.
- I can edit my own text for clarity and style.
- Lexical fields: journalism and writing; process language; academic style
- Mediation: Condense a long email into a short summary that keeps all key points.
- Skins: social, film, science, business

### B2-S14 As Much As
*Mia is choosing between two cities, two schools and two phones - but which is really better? Compare precisely, with numbers and evidence.*
- I can compare things precisely and describe how they change together.
- I can describe a chart and draw a clear conclusion.
- Lexical fields: data, charts and trends; cities and lifestyle; technology and cost
- Mediation: Explain a chart or table from a text to a friend who cannot see it.
- Skins: business, tech, travel, science, fitness

### B2-S15 Say It Clearly
*Two exchange students speak English but not each other's culture. Vee asks you and Ava to be the bridge: help them understand and decide together.*
- I can summarise and relay information from one source or speaker to another.
- I can help others understand each other by explaining and checking.
- Lexical fields: intercultural communication; meetings and teamwork; reporting and summarising
- Mediation: Run a mediation between two speakers with different views and produce a neutral summary.
- Skins: travel, business, social, music

### B2-S16 Debate League
*The semi-finals of Vee's debate league need a speaker who can argue either side. Show you can do both.*
- I can present a clear, well-structured argument and respond to questions.
- I can link ideas into a cohesive text using a range of devices.
- Lexical fields: debate language; society and ethics; cohesion and signposting
- Mediation: Chair a short discussion: summarise each speaker and invite responses.
- Skins: social, tech, business, nature, film
