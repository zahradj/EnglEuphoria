# Academy curriculum — A2

> Generated. 14 Seasons × 8 = **112 live lessons** of 60 minutes (cumulative live hours from Pre-A1: **248**). Plan rule: every lesson has an approved one-to-one plan first (`docs/academy-lesson-plan-template.md`) with research logged in `docs/research/`.

## 1. Level at a glance
- **CEFR:** A2 (paraphrase): understands frequently used expressions on immediate topics; handles simple routine exchanges; describes in simple terms background, immediate environment and past events.
- **The student:** A student who can manage the basics and now needs past, future, comparison and obligation to talk about real life.
- **Method:** Still model-and-rule, with half frames; one structure per Season (two at most); present perfect held to B1; going to and will in separate Seasons.
- **Supports:** Half frames, tiered hints, audio 0.9x on request.
- **Input texts:** ~80-150 words [target].
- **Daily 10:** 10 minutes of retrieval plus a short text; one voice note per week.

## 4. Every Season has the same eight lessons
Cold Open (hook, gist) · Word Lab (vocabulary and pronunciation) · Pattern Lab (grammar noticing) · On Air (real exchange) · Deep Dive (longer text and short writing) · Side Quest (student's pick, light new language) · Remix (zero new language) · Finale (peer task, Release, checkpoint, zero new language). Run of show: standard 60 minutes (Pre-A1 uses its own slower one).

## 5. Pronunciation and L1 priorities
- Past -ed endings /t/ /d/ /id/
- Irregular verb forms
- Article use
- Question word order (Turkish, Arabic)
- Stress in compound nouns

Season pronunciation focus:
- A2-S01: -ed endings /t/ /d/ /ɪd/; was / were in fast speech (weak forms)
- A2-S02: vowel changes in irregular verbs (go / went, buy / bought); did you ... linking /dɪdʒu/ (listening focus)
- A2-S03: weak "than" /ðən/; word stress in adjectives (expensive, comfortable)
- A2-S04: "going to" reduced to /ˈɡəʊɪŋ tə/ in connected speech (listening); sentence stress in plans
- A2-S05: stress in body-part and symptom words (stomach, headache); should / shouldn't: the weak vowel
- A2-S06: "mustn't" - silent t and final cluster; sentence stress in rules
- A2-S07: stress in food words (chocolate, vegetable); linking in "a lot of"
- A2-S08: weak "to" /tə/ in "I go there to study"; number and time stress in announcements
- A2-S09: contracted 'll /l/ ('I'll, she'll); won't vs want (vowel length)
- A2-S10: was / were /wəz/ /wə/ in connected speech; stress in two-syllable weather words
- A2-S11: -ly adverb stress (carefully, quickly); rising tone for yes/no questions
- A2-S12: intonation in polite suggestions (Let's go! / Shall we ...?); dates: ordinal numbers stress
- A2-S13: polite rising intonation on Could you ...? / Can I ...?; weak "as ... as" /əz/ in connected speech
- A2-S14: stress on the first syllable of SOMEbody / ANYthing / NOthing; present simple -s vs present continuous -ing endings

### A2-S01 Last Weekend
*Ava posted photos from a weekend nobody remembers her having. Piece together what really happened.*
**Big question:** What did I do last weekend, and where was I?
- I can say what I did and where I was at a time in the past.
- I can understand a short story about past events.

| Lesson | Chapter | Sub-question | One difficulty that rises |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **The missing weekend** | What really happened in the weekend Ava posted about? | Recognise: follow the story of a past weekend and catch the gist |
| E2 | **Weekend words** | Which words go with a weekend, a place and a time in the past? | Controlled: say and use weekend, place and time-in-the-past words |
| E3 | **Was and were** | How do I say where I was and how I felt in the past? | Controlled: build correct sentences with was and were |
| E4 | **Tell me what you did** | How do I ask a friend what they did and tell the order of events? | Guided: a short past-events exchange with a partner, using first, then, after that |
| E5 | **Whose photos are true?** | What do the details in a longer weekend story tell me about the past? | More input: a longer story, then relay the key past events |
| E6 | **My weekend, my world** | What did I do last weekend in the world I chose? | Free choice: my own past events in my own skin, my own words |
| E7 | **Remix: past or now?** | Can I talk about the past weekend without any help? | Fluency on known language only: the boss round, no new words |
| E8 | **The voice note** | What did I do last weekend, and where was I? | Release: the hardest task, a spoken story of my weekend in order |

- Lexical fields: weekend activities; time expressions for the past; places and events; feelings
- Skins: social, football, music, travel

**Big question:** How do I ask politely and choose what to buy?
- I can ask for things, for permission and for help politely (Can I ...? Could you ...? May I ...?).
- I can compare two prices or products (as cheap as, not as good as).
- I can understand a short text about pocket money and part-time jobs.

|---|---|---|---|

| E2 | **Saving and spending** | Which words go together for pocket money, part-time jobs and online shopping? | Controlled: say and use money, saving and shopping words |

### A2-S14 Stay Safe Online
*Theo's account sends strange messages that he never wrote. Vee and Ava follow the clues, one rule at a time, to find out who knows his password and how to lock the door again.*
**Big question:** What are my five rules for staying safe online?
- I can say that nobody, somebody or nothing is true or not (There is nothing wrong. Nobody knows my password.).
- I can say what I usually do online and what I am doing right now.
- I can give simple rules and advice about passwords and privacy.

| Lesson | Chapter | Sub-question | One difficulty that rises |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Strange messages** | Who is sending messages from Theo's account? | Recognise: understand the gist of a story about a hacked account |
| E2 | **Accounts and privacy** | Which words go together for accounts, passwords, photos and online problems? | Controlled: say and use account, password and privacy words that go together |
| E3 | **Nobody knows** | How do I say that somebody, nobody or nothing is true? | Controlled: build correct sentences with somebody, nobody, anything and nothing |
| E4 | **Always or right now?** | How do I say what I usually do online and what is happening now? | Guided: contrasting habits and now with a partner, in present simple and continuous |
| E5 | **Lock the door again** | What do the details in a longer text about online safety say? | More input: a longer safety text, then relay the rules and the help it suggests |
| E6 | **My online world** | What rules and advice fit the online world I chose? | Free choice: my own safety advice in my own skin |
| E7 | **Remix: the safe account** | Can I give rules, describe habits and ask for help with no help? | Fluency on known language only: the boss round, no new words |
| E8 | **Five safe rules** | What are my five rules for staying safe online? | Release: the hardest task, a short guide of five rules, spoken or written, and the level check |

- Lexical fields: accounts, passwords and privacy; sharing photos and messages; online rules and problems
- Skins: social, tech, gaming, film
