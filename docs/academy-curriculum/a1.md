# Academy curriculum — A1

> Generated. 12 Seasons × 8 = **96 live lessons** of 60 minutes (cumulative live hours from Pre-A1: **136**). Plan rule: every lesson has an approved one-to-one plan first (`docs/academy-lesson-plan-template.md`) with research logged in `docs/research/`.

## 1. Level at a glance
- **CEFR:** A1 (paraphrase): understands and uses familiar everyday expressions and very basic phrases; introduces self and others; asks and answers simple questions about personal details.
- **The student:** A teen who has met hello, numbers and countries at school: A1 revises these fast and spends the time on what school did not teach. Never childish.
- **Method:** Model first (I do / we do / you do), rule stated plainly before practice, student teach-back. Mission with Take 1 / Take 2. Eight-episode Season: Cold Open, Word Lab, Pattern Lab, On Air, Deep Dive, Side Quest, Remix, Finale.
- **Supports:** Maximum: full sentence frames, home-language hint, audio 0.8x.
- **Input texts:** ~30-60 words [target].
- **Daily 10:** 10 minutes of spaced retrieval and one speaking task (voice note).

## 4. Every Season has the same eight lessons
Cold Open (hook, gist) · Word Lab (vocabulary and pronunciation) · Pattern Lab (grammar noticing) · On Air (real exchange) · Deep Dive (longer text and short writing) · Side Quest (student's pick, light new language) · Remix (zero new language) · Finale (peer task, Release, checkpoint, zero new language). Run of show: standard 60 minutes (Pre-A1 uses its own slower one).

## 5. Pronunciation and L1 priorities
- Plural endings /s/ /z/ /iz/
- /θ/ vs /s/ (listen first)
- be-verb omission (Arabic, Turkish)
- Subject pronoun dropping (Spanish, Italian, Turkish)
- False friends

Season pronunciation focus:
- A1-S01: the alphabet (letter names that sound alike: e/i, b/p); word stress in country names
- A1-S02: plural endings /s/ /z/ /iz/; the /ɪ/ vs /iː/ contrast (it / eat)
- A1-S03: -ing endings; sentence stress on key words
- A1-S04: the /θ/ and /s/ in "thanks / sandwich" (listen first); rising tone in polite questions
- A1-S05: third-person -s endings /s/ /z/ /iz/; the /h/ sound in "he / has / hour"
- A1-S06: the /ð/ sound in "mother, father, brother" (aim for intelligible); stress in adjective + noun phrases
- A1-S07: stress in compound nouns (bus stop, car park); clear final consonants (turn, left)
- A1-S08: can /kən/ vs can't /kɑːnt/ (the vowel carries the meaning); the /ŋ/ in "singing, dancing"
- A1-S09: teens vs tens (13/30, 14/40) - stress on the right syllable; the /ɪ/ vs /iː/ contrast (this / these)
- A1-S10: -ing and weak "are" in "What are you doing?"; falling intonation in wh-questions
- A1-S11: Wednesday /ˈwenzdeɪ/ and Thursday /θ/ (silent d; th awareness for fr, tr, it); weak "to" /tə/ in "want to / need to"
- A1-S12: a vs an linked to the next word ("an_old_watch"); the /h/ in him / her (fr speakers drop it) and the vowel in them / us

### A1-S01 Who Are You?
*A fake profile has appeared in Ava's group chat. Who made it - and can you spot what is wrong with it?*
**Big question:** How can I introduce myself and ask someone the same?
- I can say hello, say my name, age and country, and ask someone the same.
- I can spell my name and read a short online profile.

| Lesson | Chapter | Sub-question | One difficulty that rises |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **The fake profile** | Which details in these online profiles look wrong? | Recognise: read and hear a profile and spot the odd detail (gist, names and countries known from school) |
| E2 | **What goes on a profile?** | Which personal information words and numbers do I need for a profile? | Controlled: say and use the words with picture and first-language support |
| E3 | **Am, is or are?** | How do I say who I am and answer What and Where questions as chunks? | Controlled: build correct sentences with be in full sentence frames |
| E4 | **Say hello, ask back** | How do I use greeting words, introducing myself and asking someone the same? | Guided: a short greeting exchange with a partner, frames still visible |
| E5 | **Spell it out** | What do longer online profiles and a spelling of a name tell me about a person? | More input: a longer profile and a spelled-out name, find the exact details |
| E6 | **My profile, my world** | Which online profiles and personal information do I want to show in the world I chose? | Free choice: my own profile details in my own words, with the frames as a floor |
| E7 | **Remix: who is who?** | Can I use every greeting, number and question so far without help? | Fluency on known language only: quick profile questions, no frames |
| E8 | **My profile card** | How can I introduce myself and ask someone the same? | Release: the hardest task, a profile card plus a 30-second voice introduction |

- Lexical fields: personal information; countries and nationalities; numbers 0-20; the alphabet; classroom language
- Skins: gaming, music, football, social

*Ava runs the school lost-and-found box and nothing has a label. Theo swears the black bag is his. Describe each object and work out whose it is.*
**Big question:** How can I describe a lost object, say whose it is and give it back?
- I can name and describe my things using a, an, the and plural nouns.
- I can ask whose something is, and use me, him, her, us and them correctly.

| Lesson | Chapter | Sub-question | One difficulty that rises |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **The box with no labels** | Which object in the lost-and-found box does each person describe? | Recognise: hear descriptions and match them to objects in the box (gist) |
| E2 | **Bags, watches, sizes** | Which belongings, materials and shape words go together in one description? | Controlled: say and use belonging, material and shape words that share one object |
| E3 | **A bag, an old watch** | How do I name my things with a, an, the and plurals? | Controlled: build correct noun phrases with articles and regular and irregular plurals |
| E4 | **Whose is it?** | How do I ask whose something is and answer with me, him, her, us or them? | Guided: a short whose-is-it exchange with a partner using object pronouns |
| E5 | **Match owner to object** | What do the details of a longer lost-property description tell me about the owner? | More input: a longer report, then decide whose object it is from the details |
| E6 | **My belongings, my angle** | What belongings would I describe in the world I chose? | Free choice: my own object and description in my own words |
| E7 | **Remix: claim it back** | Can I describe and return an object without help? | Fluency on known language only: a describe-and-return round, no frames |
| E8 | **File the report** | How can I describe a lost object, say whose it is and give it back? | Release: the hardest task, a lost-property report as a short spoken role-play, with the A1 level check |

- Lexical fields: personal belongings; materials, shapes and sizes; giving and returning things
- Skins: tech, fashion, gaming, travel
