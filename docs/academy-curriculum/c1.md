# Academy curriculum — C1

> Generated. 16 Seasons × 8 = **128 live lessons** of 60 minutes (cumulative live hours from Pre-A1: **584**). Plan rule: every lesson has an approved one-to-one plan first (`docs/academy-lesson-plan-template.md`) with research logged in `docs/research/`.

## 1. Level at a glance
- **CEFR:** C1 (paraphrase): understands demanding, longer texts and implicit meaning; expresses ideas fluently and spontaneously; uses language flexibly for social, academic and professional purposes; produces clear, well-structured, detailed text on complex subjects.
- **The student:** An advanced teen (15-18 realistic) preparing for study or work in English; school-safe topics only.
- **Method:** Language Lab (E3) notices register, collocation and stance in authentic text; E4 is a seminar, pitch or interview; mediation every Season.
- **Supports:** Off.
- **Input texts:** ~500-800 words [target].
- **Daily 10:** 10 minutes plus an extensive reading/listening target.

## 4. Every Season has the same eight lessons
Cold Open (hook, gist) · Word Lab (vocabulary and pronunciation) · Pattern Lab (grammar noticing) · On Air (real exchange) · Deep Dive (longer text and short writing) · Side Quest (student's pick, light new language) · Remix (zero new language) · Finale (peer task, Release, checkpoint, zero new language). Run of show: standard 60 minutes (Pre-A1 uses its own slower one).

## 5. Pronunciation and L1 priorities
- Prosody and focus
- Collocation precision
- Register shifts
- Cohesion in long texts

Season pronunciation focus:
- C1-S01: emphatic stress and pause after the fronted element; pace and breath in a speech
- C1-S02: intonation of formal hypothesising; contrastive stress in conditionals
- C1-S03: stress and intonation that signal doubt; rhythm of long formal sentences
- C1-S04: formal intonation and pace; avoiding reduced forms in formal speech
- C1-S05: stress in long noun phrases; rhythm in academic reading aloud
- C1-S06: dramatic pacing and stress; pause and release in a climax
- C1-S07: rhythm in participle-based sentences; stress on the main clause
- C1-S08: intonation that shows attitude; stress on stance adverbials
- C1-S09: formal vs informal pronunciation and pace; tone of voice and politeness
- C1-S10: intonation of concession (rise then fall); stress on the contrast
- C1-S11: weak and strong forms in modal chains; intonation of judgement
- C1-S12: stress and rhythm in idioms; connected speech in fixed expressions
- C1-S13: clear signposting intonation; neutral and diplomatic tone
- C1-S14: pace, pause and emphasis in speeches; rhetorical intonation (lists of three, rhetorical questions)
- C1-S15: intonation that signals irony; timing and pause in humour
- C1-S16: sustained clear delivery; signposting in long presentations

## 11. Season details
### C1-S01 Rarely Do We...
*Vee challenges everyone to open a speech so strongly that nobody looks at their phone. Which opening works - and why?*
- I can use inversion and fronting to add emphasis in speech and writing, and judge when it is too much.
- I can analyse how a speaker uses structure to persuade.
- Lexical fields: rhetoric and public speaking; rare and notable events; formal vocabulary
- Mediation: Condense a long speech into three key points for someone preparing a response.
- Skins: social, film, music, business

### C1-S02 Had I Known
*Theo's mock trial at school has a verdict that hinges on one 'if'. Argue what would have happened - in the formal register a court expects.*
- I can speculate about past and present possibilities using inverted and mixed conditionals.
- I can adapt register for a formal audience.
- Lexical fields: law, fairness and justice; formal reasoning; causes and consequences
- Mediation: Explain the key arguments of both sides of a case to a neutral listener.
- Skins: film, business, social, science

### C1-S03 They Say...
*Mia shows the group a famous image and says it was never real. Evaluate the claim, the sources and the way the story is told.*
- I can report claims impersonally and evaluate how reliable a source is.
- I can write a clear analysis of a news story.
- Lexical fields: media and misinformation; research and evidence; trust and credibility
- Mediation: Summarise a complex article for a friend and flag which claims are weak.
- Skins: social, tech, film, science

### C1-S04 I Suggest That...
*Vee asks the student council, led by Ava, for a formal proposal to change one school rule. Draft it so well that nobody can say no.*
- I can make formal recommendations and state preferences precisely.
- I can write a proposal with a clear structure and suitable tone.
- Lexical fields: school and community change; rules and policy; formal collocations
- Mediation: Turn a student's informal complaint into a polite formal request.
- Skins: business, social, nature, tech

### C1-S05 In Other Words
*Vee hands you an academic paragraph that nobody can follow. Rewrite it so a smart 14-year-old understands - and keep the meaning.*
- I can use nominalisation and complex noun phrases to write concisely and formally.
- I can move between formal and plain versions of the same idea.
- Lexical fields: academic vocabulary; science and research; abstract nouns
- Mediation: Re-express a dense academic paragraph for a general audience.
- Skins: science, tech, nature, business

### C1-S06 What Matters Is...
*Ava says every great story hides one sentence that changes everything. Find it, and learn how the author makes you notice it.*
- I can use cleft and fronted structures to shape what the reader notices.
- I can write a short story with controlled effect.
- Lexical fields: literature and film; emotion and mood; narrative devices
- Mediation: Explain the effect of a famous passage to a friend who found it confusing.
- Skins: film, anime, music, art

### C1-S07 Having Said That
*Ava submits a sentence that is 62 words long and correct. Vee says it is still bad. Why - and how do you fix it?*
- I can use perfect participle clauses and reduced relatives to write more economically.
- I can edit complex sentences for clarity and style.
- Lexical fields: writing and editing; journalism and essays; cause, sequence and result
- Mediation: Edit another student's text so it keeps meaning but is shorter and clearer.
- Skins: social, film, science, business

### C1-S08 Reading Between the Lines
*Theo sends a message: 'Admittedly, it was not a disaster.' What is he really saying? Decode the stance behind eight short messages.*
- I can understand implied attitude and express my own stance with precision.
- I can hedge and qualify claims appropriately.
- Lexical fields: attitude and opinion; critical reading; evidence and argument
- Mediation: Tell a listener what an author really thinks, based on a passage.
- Skins: social, film, music, science

### C1-S09 Formal or Not?
*Mia sends the same request to a friend, a teacher and a company. Rewrite it three times - and spot why one of the replies backfires.*
- I can adjust register to audience and purpose in speech and writing.
- I can recognise when a formal or informal choice is inappropriate.
- Lexical fields: formal and informal vocabulary; email and messaging conventions; workplace and school communication
- Mediation: Relay an informal message in a formal register to a third party.
- Skins: business, social, tech, fashion

### C1-S10 Be That As It May
*Ava's argument is nearly right. Learn how to say 'granted' without giving up your own point.*
- I can concede a point while keeping my position.
- I can structure a balanced argument with advanced concession.
- Lexical fields: argument and persuasion; ethics and society; balanced judgement
- Mediation: Help two people with opposing views find the common ground in a text.
- Skins: social, film, business, nature

### C1-S11 Needn't Have
*Theo packed for a storm that never came. Was he foolish, careful or simply unlucky? Judge with exactly the right modal.*
- I can use advanced modal forms to express obligation, expectation and judgement precisely.
- I can adjust the force of what I say with modal nuance.
- Lexical fields: planning and risk; travel and outdoors; work and responsibility
- Mediation: Explain to a friend what a set of rules really requires, which parts are optional, and which are not.
- Skins: travel, fitness, business, nature

### C1-S12 Words That Fit
*Vee asks: why 'heavy rain' but 'strong wind'? Hunt for the invisible rules behind natural English and break at least one on purpose.*
- I can use collocations and idioms precisely and avoid awkward word choices.
- I can replace repeated words with natural alternatives.
- Lexical fields: collocations and idioms; topic vocabulary in a chosen field; phrasal verbs and fixed expressions
- Mediation: Paraphrase a text using different but natural vocabulary for a different audience.
- Skins: music, film, football, social, business

### C1-S13 Mediating Worlds
*Two teams from different countries must agree a joint project, and Vee picks you as the only person who understands both. Make it work.*
- I can mediate between speakers with different viewpoints and summarise key decisions.
- I can convey the essence of long texts and discussions clearly.
- Lexical fields: intercultural communication; meetings and negotiation; international projects
- Mediation: Chair a mediation between two parties and produce a neutral written summary.
- Skins: business, travel, social, science

### C1-S14 The Art of Persuasion
*Vee shows that the best speeches use three tricks you already know. Spot them in ten clips and learn to use them ethically.*
- I can use rhetorical devices to persuade and recognise when others use them.
- I can deliver a structured, persuasive presentation.
- Lexical fields: advertising and marketing; speeches and campaigns; ethics of persuasion
- Mediation: Analyse an advert for a younger audience and explain how it tries to persuade.
- Skins: business, social, film, music

### C1-S15 Wit and Irony
*Theo says 'Not bad at all' - and Mia hears the opposite. Learn the signals of English humour and when not to take words literally.*
- I can recognise irony, understatement and humour, and use them tactfully.
- I can explain how tone changes meaning.
- Lexical fields: humour and satire; idioms and cultural references; tone and attitude
- Mediation: Explain a joke or ironic comment to someone from another culture.
- Skins: film, social, music, anime

### C1-S16 Capstone Portfolio
*Vee closes the programme with one question: what do you want the world to understand, and how will you say it in English?*
- I can plan, produce and present a substantial project in English on a topic I choose.
- I can reflect on my progress and set goals for continued learning.
- Lexical fields: project work and presentation; academic and professional skills; self-assessment
- Mediation: Summarise for an examiner the key insights from a complex source used in my project.
- Skins: science, business, art, tech, social, nature
