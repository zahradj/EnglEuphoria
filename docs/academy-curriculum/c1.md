# Academy curriculum — C1

> Generated. 18 Seasons × 8 = **144 live lessons** of 60 minutes (cumulative live hours from Pre-A1: **680**). Plan rule: every lesson has an approved one-to-one plan first (`docs/academy-lesson-plan-template.md`) with research logged in `docs/research/`.

## 1. Level at a glance
- **CEFR:** C1 (paraphrase): understands demanding, longer texts and implicit meaning; expresses ideas fluently and spontaneously; uses language flexibly for social, academic and professional purposes; produces clear, well-structured, detailed text on complex subjects.
- **The student:** An advanced teen (15-18 realistic) preparing for study or work in English; school-safe topics only.
- **Method:** Language Lab (E3) notices register, collocation and stance in authentic text; E4 is a seminar, pitch or interview; mediation every Season.
- **Supports:** Off.
- **Input texts:** ~500-800 words [target].
- **Daily 10:** 10 minutes plus an extensive reading/listening target.

## 4. Every Season has the same eight lessons
Cold Open (hook, gist) · Word Lab (vocabulary and pronunciation) · Pattern Lab (grammar noticing) · On Air (real exchange) · Deep Dive (longer text and short writing) · Side Quest (student's pick, light new language) · Remix (zero new language) · Finale (peer task, Release, checkpoint, zero new language). Run of show: standard 60 minutes (Pre-A1 uses its own slower one).

- C1-S10: intonation of concession (rise then fall); stress on the contrast
- C1-S11: weak and strong forms in modal chains; intonation of judgement
- C1-S12: stress and rhythm in idioms; connected speech in fixed expressions
- C1-S13: clear signposting intonation; neutral and diplomatic tone

### C1-S01 Rarely Do We...
*Vee challenges everyone to open a speech so strongly that nobody looks at their phone. Which opening works - and why?*
**Big question:** How can I open a speech so strongly that people listen, and judge when it is too much?
- I can use inversion and fronting to add emphasis in speech and writing, and judge when it is too much.
- I can analyse how a speaker uses structure to persuade.

| Lesson | Chapter | Sub-question | One difficulty that rises |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **The opening that worked** | Which powerful opening makes a room listen, and why? | Recognise: follow two openings and hear the gist of the stronger one |
| E2 | **Words for the platform** | Which rhetoric and formal words belong to a speech about a rare event? | Controlled: say and use a few thematic words |
| E3 | **Rarely do we** | How does inversion after a negative adverbial work in a strong opening? | Controlled: build correct sentences with the pattern, with a model |
| E4 | **Answer with emphasis** | How do I use emphatic style to make a point with a partner in a short exchange? | Guided: a short exchange, with prompts on the screen |
| E5 | **Anatomy of a speech** | How does style and structure in a longer speech persuade, and what are its three key points? | More input: a longer speech, then relay its key points |
| E6 | **My own stage** | How do I give a powerful opening in the world I chose? | Free choice: my own topic and my own words, little planning |
| E7 | **Remix: too much?** | Can I use emphatic style, and judge when it is too much, without help? | Fluency on known language only: no new items, no help |
| E8 | **A 90-second opening** | How can I open a speech so strongly that people listen, and judge when it is too much? | Release: the hardest task, a spoken opening plus a reflection on its effect |

- Lexical fields: rhetoric and public speaking; rare and notable events; formal vocabulary
- Mediation: Condense a long speech into three key points for someone preparing a response.
- Skins: social, film, music, business
