# Reading Engine

## Purpose

Manage literacy progression across levels so a learner never gets the same
reading instruction twice at different CEFR levels, and never gets reading
content pitched at the wrong stage for their level.

## Reading progression by level — check this BEFORE writing phonics content

| Level | Goal | Teach | Do NOT teach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-A1 | Letter & sound discovery | Letter recognition, primary letter sounds, phonological awareness, CVC blending | Paragraph reading, grammar-heavy reading |
| A1 | Decode simple words/sentences | CVC review *through reading*, high-frequency words, short sentences, simple dialogues | Reteaching the alphabet (unless remediation) |
| A2 | Reading fluency | Longer texts, silent e, long vowels, story reading, comprehension | — |
| B1 | Strategic reading | Skimming, scanning, inference, pronunciation support, connected texts | — |
| B2 | Independent reading | Authentic texts, critical reading, tone, vocabulary in context, analysis | — |

The A1 row matters most for this project right now: **A1 assumes the
learner already has Pre-A1's letter-sound discovery** — an A1 phonics scene
should be framed as review-through-reading ("remember this sound? now read
it in a word"), not as first-time discovery ("here is what S sounds like").
A sound-model scene that plays like a Pre-A1 lesson, just relabeled A1, is
teaching at the wrong stage even if the letters themselves are new to that
specific lesson's word list.

## Reading stages (finer-grained than CEFR level alone)

0. Pre-reader → 1. Letter Discovery → 2. Sound Awareness → 3. Blending →
4. Decoding → 5. Sentence Reading → 6. Fluent Reading → 7. Strategic Reading

A lesson's phonics content should identify which stage it's actually
operating at and confirm that stage is appropriate for the target CEFR
level's row above — don't default to "Letter Discovery" framing just because
a letter happens to be new within this specific lesson.

## Decision rule

1. Determine the CEFR level.
2. Determine the reading stage that level is responsible for (table above).
3. Check what's already mastered (earlier lessons in the same unit/level).
4. Select the next objective *at that stage*, not a lower one.
5. Generate activities that match — decoding/reading practice for A1, not
   letter-introduction rituals.
6. Validate against repetition (don't reteach what an earlier level already
   owns).

## Quality checklist

- [ ] The reading objective matches the CEFR row above, not a lower stage
- [ ] No unnecessary repetition of a *different* level's already-owned content
- [ ] Reading builds on the previous lesson in the same unit's knowledge graph
- [ ] Activities align with the decoding/fluency goal for this specific level
- [ ] Any story used supports the reading objective, not just the vocabulary
